# Sneaker price comparison pages for a small Indian reseller: business case and buyer behaviour (as of 2026-11-21)

Method note: about 16 tool calls. Rate cards were read on the page (labelled "verified, page opened"). Category payouts on Cuelinks are gated behind sign-up, so NO footwear-specific rate was seen for any store except where stated. Search snippets and press are "secondary". Nothing was found for several questions; see each Gaps list.

## 1. Affiliate programmes in India (rates, cookies, rules)

### Takeaway
Footwear-specific commission is NOT publicly visible for Myntra, Ajio and Tata CLiQ (gated), and only headline ranges of about 4.5 to 10.8 percent are public. Cookies are short (Myntra 1 day, Ajio 1 day, Tata CLiQ 7 days). No page opened mentions price-comparison traffic as allowed or banned, so it needs written confirmation. Of the sneaker boutiques, only Superkicks (paused) and VegNonVeg have network listings; Culture Circle, Crepdog Crew, Mainstreet, Dawntown, HypeFly, Limited Edt, Foot Locker India, Nike India, Adidas India, Puma India, New Balance India: not found.

### Inferences
- A comparison page that links to a rival retailer and wants payment must qualify under each programme's media list. "Comparison" is not named in any opened list, but "Coupons/Deals" is, and Ajio/Superkicks restrict coupon payouts to Cuelinks-supplied codes. Written approval from each network is the only safe route.
- Being a shoe retailer is not mentioned in any opened rule, but nothing says it is allowed either.
- Cookie of 1 day (Myntra, Ajio) means only same-day purchases pay; research-heavy Rs 10,000+ purchases will often fall outside it. This is an inference.

### Gaps

- EarnKaro, INRDeals, vCommission, Admitad, Impact, Optimise rate cards not opened; Amazon cookie window and comparison rule not read.

## 2. Unit economics of comparison and deal sites

### Takeaway
No click-out rate, post-click conversion or earnings per 1,000 visits was found for any Indian comparison site. Only the revenue model and traffic estimates exist. The break-even arithmetic below is illustrative and uses stated assumptions.

### Cited Findings
- MySmartPrice says it earns affiliate commissions and ads from retailers such as Amazon and Flipkart; one profile says over 80 percent of revenue is affiliate (secondary, approximate). — [MySmartPrice about](https://www.mysmartprice.com/about/); [StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/mysmartprice-success-story/)
- 2014 Business Standard: phones about 70 percent and electronics 15 percent of MySmartPrice revenue (dated, 12 years old). — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/companies/of-price-comparison-discounts-and-deals-114092900069_1.html)
- 91mobiles expected about $2.8 Mn revenue in FY17-18 after merging with Pricebaba (old). — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/smartphone-discovery-platform-91mobiles-acquires-majority-stake-in-pricebaba-com)
- Traffic estimates (SEMrush, 2025, estimates): 91mobiles 8.25M visits, MySmartPrice 3.35M, Smartprix 15.22M per month in different snapshots. — [SEMrush 91mobiles](https://cw.semrush.com/website/91mobiles.com/competitors); [SEMrush MySmartPrice](https://cw.semrush.com/website/mysmartprice.com/competitors)

### Inferences (arithmetic, assumptions flagged)
- Assume a Rs 10,000 order and the visible commission band of about 4.5 to 5.4 percent (Ajio/Superkicks/Tata CLiQ headline lows, not footwear-verified): about Rs 450 to 540 per order. Rs 10,000 a month then needs roughly 19 to 22 validated orders. At the Myntra existing-user 3.75 percent it is about 27 orders.
- If 1 to 3 percent of click-outs became validated orders (ASSUMPTION, no Indian source), that is about 640 to 2,200 click-outs a month. If 30 to 50 percent of visitors click out (ASSUMPTION), that is roughly 1,300 to 7,300 qualified comparison-intent visits. The store's whole organic traffic is about 900 clicks a month, so even a good outcome needs a multiple of current traffic, with 60 to 90 day validation and 30 to 60 day payment lag (per Cuelinks pages above).

### Gaps
- Real click-out rates, EPC and conversion for 91mobiles, Smartprix, MySmartPrice, CashKaro/EarnKaro, Desidime: not found. Global benchmarks not searched.

## 3. Retailer-as-comparator evidence

### Takeaway
Progressive is the classic case, but no source with measured sales or conversion results was found. Academic evidence is about consumers who use comparison sites shifting away from branded retailers, which argues against the store being the one to push comparisons unless it is cheapest.

### Cited Findings
- Progressive showed competitor rates on its homepage, even when it was not lowest (GEICO $36 cheaper in the example); author argues customers compare anyway so showing it keeps them on site. This is a 2005 commentary with no results data (US, context only). — [Finovate/Jim Bruene](https://finovate.com/progressive-insurance-quotes-competitors-rates/)
- ClickZ commentary claims showing competitor prices makes consumers focus less on price (opinion). — [ClickZ](https://clickz.com/coping-with-price-transparency/67821/)
- Waldfogel and Chen (Journal of Industrial Economics, 1998-99 data, US): adopters of price comparison sites cut shopping at a broad group of branded retailers by about a tenth (via search snippet; paper not opened). — [NBER w9942 listing](https://nber.org/papers/w9942)
- Bodur, Klein, Arora, Journal of Retailing 2015 (context): a favourable rating of a comparison-site retailer raises the weight shoppers give its price when judging offline retailers. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jouret:v:91:y:2015:i:1:p:125-139)
- Bohlmann and Kocas, Journal of Marketing 2008: with many switchers comparing prices, big stores should limit themselves to a few small discounts (theory, summary only). — [UW-Madison summary](https://business.wisc.edu/news/is-that-a-good-price-how-online-research-affects-in-person-purchases/)

### Inferences
- Progressive's model works for a firm whose price is competitive on a large share of quotes and which holds a strong brand; 54 of 85 benchmarked items sitting 5 percent or more above Google's benchmark means the store would lose most visible comparisons. This is judgement, not a measured result.

### Gaps
- Progressive conversion data, Indian retailer examples, Amazon buy-box effects, price-match guarantee studies, travel/electronics cases: not found within budget.

## 4. Indian sneaker buyer behaviour

### Takeaway
Authenticity is the dominant, repeatedly stated concern; price is secondary and Indian prices are legitimately inflated by import duty. No survey quantifying the price gap that makes a buyer switch, or the frequency of "is X legit" searches, was found.

### Cited Findings
- Culture Circle co-founder Ackshay Jain: for sneakerheads in India the greatest concern has always been counterfeits; platform uses dual authentication with CheckCheck, a digital authenticity certificate, "guaranteed lowest prices" and money-back policy (founder claims, secondary). — [DQ India interview](https://www.dqindia.com/interview/we-are-creating-a-truly-international-standard-of-verification-culture-circle-9083308); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/02/culture-circle-bridging-trust-gap-india-luxury-resale-market)
- Inc42 (4 Apr 2025): India sneaker market projected $3.8 Bn FY24 to $5.9 Bn FY32 (5.45 percent CAGR, a cited report); Find Your Kicks AOV Rs 10,000 to 11,000, 2,000 to 2,500 pairs a month; Hustle Culture AOV Rs 10,000 to 13,000, about 500 pairs a month (March 2025); Comet AOV Rs 4,000 to 6,000; Crepdog Crew three stores, 50-50 online/offline; counterfeits "plague the supply chain" and some resale platforms were criticised for fakes. VegNonVeg and Superkicks not in the article. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/how-sneaker-resellers-d2c-brands-are-cashing-in-on-indias-6-bn-boom/)
- Community guidance (secondary, many authors are resellers): check a store's name on r/SneakersIndia, Instagram and Twitter for a mix of praise and answered complaints; confirm return window, how authentication works, and COD availability; a recent r/SneakersIndia snippet asks whether a Flipkart seller is safe (anecdote). — [KicksMachine guide](https://www.kicksmachine.com/blogs/news/verified-sneakers-online-in-india-how-to-buy-without-getting-burned); [Hustle Culture](https://hustleculture.co.in/blogs/articles/is-buying-premium-sneakers-from-india-based-stores-safe); [r/SneakersIndia mirror](https://redlib.belloworld.it/r/SneakersIndia/new)
- Hustle Culture notes import duty is baked into Indian prices of international pairs, so a higher price alone does not signal fake (store self-interest). — [Hustle Culture](https://hustleculture.co.in/blogs/articles/is-buying-premium-sneakers-from-india-based-stores-safe)

### Inferences
- For a new domain with 2 failed-payment orders, trust signals probably matter more than a Rs 500 to 1,000 price gap, but no study here confirms the switching threshold.

### Gaps
- Quantified switching thresholds, "is <store> legit" search volume, delivery/returns/EMI/COD preference data, Quora and YouTube comment patterns, Superkicks and Mainstreet interviews: not found.

## 5. How buyers find deals; alternatives to head-to-head comparison

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings

## 6. Counter-risks

### Takeaway
No sourced case was found; the risks below are reasoning from the findings.

### Inferences
- With 54 of 85 benchmarked items above benchmark (owner's data), the page would mostly rank the store below competitors on its own domain.
- Nine of the eleven named stores either lack a visible affiliate programme or have gated or paused ones, so naming them yields little income while sending scarce visitors (about 900 clicks a month) to them.
- Naming brands carries trade-mark and accuracy exposure if prices are stale; not researched.
- Affiliate terms on three networks restrict social and paid traffic, so growth would depend on organic search alone.

### Gaps
- Any documented undercutting, brand-perception study, or marketplace-shown-as-expensive case: not found.
