# Google ranking systems and policies for blog, news and reference content (2025-2026), with AI-content policy and small-publisher levers

Research date: 2026-10-07. Scope: what a small Indian sneaker e-commerce blog (release news, drop calendars, model guides) must satisfy or can exploit to outrank Sneaker News, Hypebeast, Sole Retriever and Indian competitors. Anything older than 2025 is marked "(older)". Google-stated facts are quoted with URLs; SEO-community inference is labelled as such.

Primary-source note: the September 11, 2025 Search Quality Rater Guidelines PDF (182 pages) was downloaded and text-extracted locally, so all QRG quotes below are verbatim from the current edition.

## KQ1. State of the Helpful Content system, E-E-A-T guidance, and what the Quality Rater Guidelines (Sept 2025 edition) say about AI content, "Lowest" pages, effort and originality

### Takeaway
The helpful content system no longer exists as a separate system; since March 2024 it is folded into core ranking, so every core update (four in 2025-2026 so far) re-scores "helpfulness". The current QRG (September 11, 2025) explicitly says generative AI use alone does not set the rating, but pages whose main content is "all or almost all" AI-generated, paraphrased or copied with "little to no effort, little to no originality, and little to no added value" must be rated Lowest, and raters are told to apply Lowest to suspected scaled content "no matter how it's created".

### Cited Findings
- Google's ranking systems guide (last updated 2025-12-10) lists the helpful content system under retired systems: "Originally announced in 2022; became integrated into core ranking systems in March 2024." The same page lists live systems including "Freshness systems", "Original content systems" (prioritises original reporting over content merely citing it), the "Reviews system" ("Aims to better reward high quality reviews, content that provides insightful analysis and original research"), "Site diversity system" ("Generally won't show more than two web page listings from the same site in our top results"), "Local news systems" and "Spam detection systems (SpamBrain)". - [Google Search Central, ranking systems guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide)
- Google's "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" self-assessment asks verbatim: "Does the content provide original information, reporting, research, or analysis?"; "Does the content provide substantial value when compared to other pages in search results?"; "If the content draws on other sources, does it avoid simply copying or rewriting those sources?"; "Does your content clearly demonstrate first-hand expertise and a depth of knowledge (for example, expertise that comes from having actually used a product or service, or visiting a place)?" It frames E-E-A-T with trust as the most important member, and asks creators to answer "Who, How, and Why": clear bylines and author background (Who), transparency about how content was made including "automation or AI-generation use" (How), and content made "primarily to benefit people, not manipulate search rankings" (Why). It says that when automation generates substantial content, creators should disclose this and explain why it provided value. - [Google Search Central, creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- The current QRG edition is dated "September 11, 2025", 182 pages (verified from the PDF cover and page count). - [Google Search Quality Evaluator Guidelines PDF](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- QRG section 2.1 (verbatim): "Generative AI is a type of machine learning (ML) model that can take what it has learned from the examples it has been provided to create new content, such as text, images, music, and code. ... Different tools leverage these models to create generative AI content. Generative AI can be a helpful tool for content creation, but like any tool, it can also be misused." - [QRG PDF](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- QRG section 3.2, definition of Effort (verbatim): "Effort: Consider the extent to which a human being actively worked to create satisfying content. Effort may be direct, such as a person translating a poem from one language to another. Effort may go into designing page functionality or building systems that power a webpage, such as the creation of a page that offers machine translation as a service to users. On the other hand, the automatic creation of thousands of pages by running existing freely available content through existing translation software without any oversight, manual curation, etc., would not be considered to have effort." - [QRG PDF](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- QRG section 3.4, Experience and Trust (verbatim): "Experience: Consider the extent to which the content creator has the necessary first-hand or life experience for the topic. ... For example, which would you trust: a product review from someone who has personally used the product or a 'review' by someone who has not?" and "Trust is the most important member of the E-E-A-T family because untrustworthy pages have low E-E-A-T no matter how Experienced, Expert, or Authoritative they may seem." - [QRG PDF](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- QRG section 4.6.5 Scaled Content Abuse (verbatim): "Creating an abundance of content with little effort or originality with no editing or manual curation is often the defining attribute of spammy websites. ... Scaled content abuse occurs when many pages are generated for the purpose of primarily benefiting the website owner and not helping users. This practice is typically focused on creating large amounts of unoriginal content that provides little to no value for website visitors compared to other similar pages on the web, no matter how it's created." Examples include "Using automated tools (generative AI or otherwise) as a low-effort way to produce many pages that add little-to-no value for website visitors as compared to other pages on the web on the same topic", "Scraping feeds, search results, or other content to generate many pages (including through automated transformations like synonymizing, translating, or other obfuscation techniques)", "Stitching or combining content from different web pages without adding value", "Creating multiple sites with the intent of hiding the scaled nature of the content". Rating instruction: "Pages and websites made up of content created at scale with no original content or added value for users, should be rated Lowest, no matter how they are created. Even if you are unsure of the method of creation, e.g. whether or not the page is created using generative AI tools, you should still use the Lowest rating when you strongly suspect scaled content abuse after looking at several pages on the website." - [QRG PDF](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- QRG section 4.6.6 "MC Created with Little to No Effort, Little to No Originality, and Little to No Added Value for Website Visitors" (verbatim): "The Lowest rating applies if all or almost all of the MC on the page (including text, images, audio, videos, etc) is copied, paraphrased, embedded, auto or AI generated, or reposted from other sources with little to no effort, little to no originality, and little to no added value for visitors to the website. Such pages should be rated Lowest, even if the page assigns credit for the content to another source." It defines paraphrasing: "We will consider MC to be paraphrased if it contains content that is unoriginal and exists on other pages, with different wording, organization or phrasing. ... Automated tools can also be used to create paraphrased content by restating or summarizing the content on other pages." It adds: "Paraphrasing may be valuable, for example when an expert paraphrases the contents of a government policy in easy-to-understand language." - [QRG PDF](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- QRG 4.6.6, the AI-neutrality sentence (verbatim): "Important: Copying, paraphrasing, embedding or reposting content does not automatically make a page Lowest quality. However, the Lowest rating is required when all or almost all of the MC on the page ... is copied, paraphrased, embedded, or reposted with little to no effort, little to no originality, and little to no added value for website visitors. Likewise, the use of Generative AI tools alone does not determine the level of effort or Page Quality rating. Generative AI tools may be used for high quality and low quality content creation. For example, a high level of effort may be involved in creating high quality original artwork using Generative AI tools. However, it's also possible to use Generative AI tools to create Lowest quality content with little to no effort, little to no originality, and little to no added value for website visitors." - [QRG PDF](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- QRG 4.6.6 explicitly exempts syndication: "Licensed or syndicated content should not be considered as 'copied' ... Examples of syndicated content in the U.S. include news articles by AP or Reuters." - [QRG PDF](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- QRG 4.6.3 Expired Domain Abuse (verbatim): "Expired domain abuse is where an expired domain name is purchased and repurposed primarily to benefit the new website owner by hosting content that provides little to no value to users." - [QRG PDF](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- The generative AI definition and sections 4.6.5/4.6.6 first appeared in the January 2025 QRG edition; John Mueller publicly described the change at Search Central Live Madrid on April 9, 2025, saying raters are directed to watch for pages whose main content is created with automated or generative AI tools and potentially rate them Lowest. - [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-quality-raters-content-ai-generated-454161); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-updates-quality-rater-guidelines-with-ai-content-evaluation-criteria/)
- The September 2025 edition also expanded "YMYL Society" to "YMYL Government, Civics & Society" (elections, voting, trust in public institutions). - [SEO Kreativ](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/google-quality-raters-update_9-25/)
- Contradiction: SEO Kreativ claims the September 2025 QRG added evaluation criteria for AI Overviews. A text search of the extracted PDF returns zero occurrences of "AI Overview", so this claim is not supported by the current document. - [SEO Kreativ](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/google-quality-raters-update_9-25/); contradicted by the [QRG PDF](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)

### Inferences
- For snkrscart.com, the practical bar is "added value compared to other pages on the web on the same topic". A release post that restates the brand press release (which Sneaker News, Hypebeast and 50 others also restate) is by definition "paraphrased" content; the QRG only exempts paraphrase when it adds expert value (the "expert paraphrases a government policy" example). India-specific price, retailer availability, sizing and first-hand photos are the obvious ways to make a release post non-paraphrased.
- "Effort" in the QRG explicitly includes "building systems that power a webpage" and "designing page functionality". A drop calendar with live countdowns, calendar export and India-time conversion counts as effort in rater terms, which text-only competitors cannot show.
- Raters are told to judge scaled abuse "after looking at several pages on the website", so a sitewide pattern of thin AI posts can drag down the good pages; the risk is site-level, not page-level.

### Gaps
- Raters do not set rankings directly; Google has never published how rater data trains the live classifier. No source quantifies how much weight the 4.6.6 "Lowest" criterion carries in live ranking.
- I could not confirm the exact release date of the January 2025 QRG edition (reported as January 23, 2025 in secondary sources, not verified from a primary document).

## KQ2. Scaled content abuse and site reputation abuse: exact wording and observed enforcement 2024-2026

### Takeaway
Scaled content abuse is defined by purpose ("primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users") and is method-agnostic; enforcement moved from purely algorithmic (March 2024) to manual actions with full deindexing from roughly June 2025. Site reputation abuse targets third-party content riding a host's signals, with first-party oversight no longer a defence since November 19, 2024; as of August 30, 2026 the penalty no longer demotes for searchers inside the EEA, where the section is instead ranked independently.

### Cited Findings
- Spam policies page (last updated 2026-08-28), scaled content abuse (verbatim): "Scaled content abuse is when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users." Listed examples include "Using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users", scraping feeds and search results with automated transformations, stitching content from multiple sources without adding value, creating multiple sites to hide the scaled nature, and generating pages with keywords that make little sense to a reader. - [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Spam policies page, expired domain abuse (verbatim): "Expired domain abuse is where an expired domain name is purchased and repurposed primarily to manipulate search rankings by hosting content that provides little to no value to users." - [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Spam policies page, site reputation abuse core concept (verbatim): "Third-party content is published on a host site mainly because of that host's already-established ranking signals, which it has earned primarily from its first-party content." The page now notes that within the European Economic Area the affected section is ranked independently rather than demoted. - [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- November 2024 definition as reported: "Site reputation abuse is the practice of publishing third-party pages on a site in an attempt to abuse search rankings by taking advantage of the host site's ranking signals." Chris Nelson (Google Search Quality) said first-party oversight does not change the third-party nature of content, covering "white-label services, licensing agreements, and partial ownership structures". Not covered: wire-service and syndicated news, user-generated content on forums, editorial content with close host involvement, properly disclosed advertorials, standard ad units and affiliate links. Enforcement: manual actions via Search Console, with algorithmic enforcement planned. - [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-strengthens-policy-against-site-reputation-abuse/533018/); original post [Google Search Central blog, Nov 2024](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/11/site-reputation-abuse)
- Timeline (older, 2024): policy announced March 5, 2024 with the March 2024 core update; manual-action enforcement began May 7, 2024; examples expanded September 2024; clarified November 19, 2024. Named casualties: Forbes Advisor, CNN Underscored, Fortune Recommends, WSJ Buyside, Men's Journal. 2025 reports add European publisher coupon sections (focus.de, bild.de, chip.de, Le Figaro, Le Monde). - [CapConvert, "18 months in"](https://www.capconvert.com/learn/blog/google-site-reputation-abuse-18-months); [Sistrix on Forbes Advisor](https://www.sistrix.com/blog/google-tightens-site-reputation-abuse-policy-and-removes-forbes-advisor-from-the-index/)
- EEA change: effective August 30, 2026 (announced August 28, 2026), site reputation manual actions stop demoting results for searchers inside the EEA; "the affected section of the site may instead be separated within its systems so that it ranks independently from the rest of the site over time. No demotion is applied." Outside the EEA enforcement is unchanged. Google attributed the change to discussions with the European Commission, which opened Digital Markets Act proceedings in November 2025, and said it remains concerned that "an overbroad application of the Digital Markets Act could prevent it from addressing threats to search result integrity." - [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-drops-eea-search-penalties-under-site-reputation-policy-august-30/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-updates-site-reputation-abuse-policy-removes-penalties-in-eea/587429/)
- Scaled content abuse manual actions: first documented June 2-3, 2025, with Search Console messages citing "large-scale content abuse"; two actions confirmed the same day, additional APAC cases June 9, 2025; affected sites used generative AI programmatically "at mass scale in order to game Google's ranking systems"; the author states the overall scale of the wave is unknown. - [Gagan Ghotra](https://gaganghotra.com/blog/seo/scaled-content-abuse-manual-actions-google-issuing/)
- SEO-community reports (secondary) describe waves of scaled content abuse manual actions from around June 3, 2025 with complete deindexing (not ranking drops) across UK, US and EU sites, and that manual-action reports cluster around "programmatically templated pages with thin per-page value". - [Meeeters](https://www.meeeters.com/seo-automation/scaled-content-abuse); [Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/scaled-content-abuse); [Niche Pursuits](https://www.nichepursuits.com/?p=133767)
- Spam updates logged on Google's status dashboard: August 2025 spam update (started Aug 26, 2025, 26 days 15 hours), March 2026 spam update (Mar 24, 2026, 19 h 30 min), June 2026 spam update (Jun 24, 2026, 2 days 1 hour), August 2026 spam update (Aug 18, 2026, 2 days 16 hours), September 2026 spam update (started Sep 24, 2026). - [Google Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history)
- (older, 2024) Google's March 2024 announcement said the combined changes would reduce low-quality, unoriginal content in results by about 40% (later stated as 45%). - [Google blog, March 2024](https://blog.google/products/search/google-search-update-march-2024/)

### Inferences
- The scaled-content policy is about purpose and value, so volume itself is not the trigger; a drop calendar with hundreds of release pages is safe only if each page carries something not available elsewhere (India price, retailer list, raffle links, sizing run, real photos). Templated pages that only restate release date and colourway are exactly what raters and the June 2025 manual actions targeted.
- Site reputation abuse is not a risk for snkrscart.com as a host (no third-party sections), but it explains why big publishers' affiliate "best sneaker deals" subfolders lost visibility in 2024-2025, which is an opening for first-party commerce content.
- The EEA carve-out does not apply to Indian or US searchers.

### Gaps
- No Google statement gives the number of sites hit by scaled-content manual actions in 2025-2026; counts come from individual consultants.
- No verified case of an AI-assisted but human-edited niche publisher receiving a scaled content abuse manual action was found.

## KQ3. Does Google detect or penalise AI-written text per se? Google statements and independent studies

### Takeaway
Google's stated position, repeated from February 2023 through the September 2025 QRG and Danny Sullivan's January 2026 remarks, is that it judges quality and purpose, not production method. Large-scale studies disagree on whether AI text underperforms: Ahrefs (600,000 pages, 2025) found a 0.011 correlation between AI share and rank, while Graphite (31,493 keywords, 2025) found human-written pages win position one disproportionately (7% of #1 results were AI vs 14% of all results).

### Cited Findings
- (older, Feb 2023, still Google's standing guidance) "Appropriate use of AI or automation is not against our guidelines. This means that it is not used to generate content primarily to manipulate search rankings, which is against our spam policies." The post also says automation has long produced helpful content such as sports scores, weather and transcripts, and that Google's focus is on quality of content rather than how it is produced. - [Google Search Central blog](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/02/google-search-and-ai-content)
- QRG September 2025 (verbatim): "the use of Generative AI tools alone does not determine the level of effort or Page Quality rating. Generative AI tools may be used for high quality and low quality content creation." - [QRG PDF](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)
- Google AI features documentation (last updated 2025-12-10): "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary." and "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features." - [Google Search Central, AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- Danny Sullivan, January 8, 2026, on splitting content into LLM-sized chunks: "So we don't want you to do that." He said Google consulted engineers before issuing the guidance, warned "the systems may change" so system-specific tactics will not survive, and urged creators to write "for the authentic stuff". He described AEO/GEO as "a subset of SEO". - [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-warns-against-breaking-content-into-chunks-for-ai/); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-danny-sullivan-seo-for-ai-is-still-seo-466368)
- John Mueller, Search Central Live Madrid, April 9, 2025: raters are directed to watch for pages whose main content is created with automated or generative AI tools and rate them Lowest (reported). - [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-quality-raters-content-ai-generated-454161)
- Ahrefs study (published July 2025): top 20 URLs for 100,000 keywords, 600,000 pages scored with Ahrefs' detector; correlation between AI-content share and position was 0.011; over 86% of top-ranking pages contained some AI content; 4.6% entirely AI, 13.5% purely human, 81.9% mixed; position-one pages had slightly less AI content. - [Ahrefs](https://www.ahrefs.com/blog/ai-generated-content-does-not-hurt-your-google-rankings); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ahrefs-study-finds-no-evidence-google-penalizes-ai-content/550656/)
- Graphite study (published October 14, 2025; data June 2025): 31,493 keywords across 10 categories, Surfer's detector on 500-word chunks; 14% of Google results AI-generated vs 86% human; "only 7% of the articles ranking number one are AI-generated"; human pages ranked statistically significantly higher when competing for the same keyword (p < 1e-6); the study "did not evaluate the effectiveness of AI-assisted content with heavy human editing". It also found ChatGPT and Perplexity citations were 18% AI-generated. - [Graphite](https://graphite.io/five-percent/ai-content-in-search-and-llms)
- Originality.ai tracker: AI content in Google's top 20 for 500 popular keywords hit 19.10% on January 21, 2025, up from 18.07% in November 2024 (ongoing dashboard). - [Originality.ai](https://originality.ai/ai-content-in-google-search-results)
- Conflict: Ahrefs ("no correlation") vs Graphite ("human ranks higher"). The two used different detectors, different samples and different questions (position correlation across all 20 results vs share of #1 results), so both can be true: AI presence is tolerated across the top 20 while the #1 slot skews human. - [Ahrefs](https://www.ahrefs.com/blog/ai-generated-content-does-not-hurt-your-google-rankings); [Graphite](https://graphite.io/five-percent/ai-content-in-search-and-llms)
- SEO-community claim (unverified, secondary): analyses of the December 2025 core update state that "unedited AI content lost 85-95% of traffic" and affiliate sites had a 71% negative impact rate. No methodology was published. - [Growing Search](https://www.growingsearch.com/blog/december-2025-core-update/); [ThatWare](https://thatware.co/google-december-2025-core-update/)

### Inferences
- Google does not need a text-level AI detector; it uses value-relative-to-the-web signals (originality, duplication, effort, engagement). The evidence is consistent with AI drafting being safe when the page carries unique inputs, and risky when the page is a rewrite of the same press release everyone else has.
- Position one skewing human (Graphite) is consistent with the information-gain and proprietary-asset findings in KQ4: the top slot tends to go to the page with something new, which pure generation cannot supply.

### Gaps
- No Google statement or study quantifies the effect of disclosing AI assistance (the "How" of Who/How/Why) on rankings.
- No 2026 large-sample study on AI content and rankings was found; the latest large studies are mid-to-late 2025.

## KQ4. Information gain (patent) and evidence that unique data, first-hand photos and original quotes improve rankings

### Takeaway
"Information gain" comes from Google patent US 11,354,342 B2 ("Contextual estimation of link information gain", filed 2018, granted 2022), which scores a document by the information it adds beyond documents the user has already seen. Independent 2026 analyses converge on the same lever: Zyppy's 400-site study found 92.9% of core-update winners own proprietary assets (datasets, products, images, studies), and informational sites (news publishers and affiliates) lost the most.

### Cited Findings
- Patent: "Contextual estimation of link information gain", US11354342B2, filed 2018, granted 2022, inventor Victor Carbune (Google). Verbatim: "An information gain score for a given document is indicative of additional information that is included in the given document beyond information contained in other documents that were already presented to the user." The patent uses semantic comparison rather than vocabulary matching. - [Ahrefs, Louise Linehan, Aug 20, 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/information-gain/)
- Correction to the brief: the number "US 2022/0121633" given in the assignment resolves to US20220121633A1, an IBM patent on database workload optimisation, not the information gain patent. - [Google Patents US20220121633A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20220121633A1/en)
- Ahrefs' operationalisation list: original data and surveys (which it found correlate most strongly with information gain scores), first-hand experience and case studies, expert quotes not found on competing pages, tools or proprietary assets, unique angles for underserved intents, and refreshes with current information. - [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/information-gain/)
- On-Page.ai study (June 14, 2026, 150 top-ranking pages): pages with 15 or more unique data points averaged an information gain score of 62 vs 40 for pages with one or none. Search-result summaries of the same study report that drafts in the top novelty tercile reached peak position a median of 11 days sooner and earned 2.3x more unsolicited backlinks over six months, and that novelty above roughly 40% showed no extra benefit. The PDF extract I read confirmed the sample and the 62-vs-40 figure but not the 11-day and 2.3x figures. - [On-Page.ai whitepaper PDF](https://api.on-page.ai/whitepapers/information-gain-study-2026.pdf); as cited by [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/information-gain/)
- Cyrus Shepard (Zyppy Signal) study of 400 winning and losing sites over 12 months through the March 2026 core update: 92.9% of winners own proprietary assets "difficult to replicate, such as datasets, products, images, or studies"; 83.7% enable task completion; 75.9% have niche expertise; 70.2% offer a unique product or service; 32.6% have a strong brand. Spearman correlations: product/service 0.391, task completion 0.381, proprietary assets 0.357, tight topical focus 0.250, brand 0.206. Sites with four or five traits had win rates of 68.1% and 69.7% vs 13.5% for sites with none. "informational sites (news publishers and affiliate sites) lost the most traffic." - [Practical Ecommerce, Ann Smarty, Apr 20, 2026](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/organic-search-winners-share-5-traits)
- Raptive analysis of the December 2025 core update (published Feb 4, 2026): winners vs losers differed on LCP under 2.3 s, ads-to-content ratio under 25% (sites under 25% performed "four percentage points better"), thin content (winners had under 7% of pages at 500 words or fewer vs 32%+ for losers), branded search share (above 4% of clicks vs below 0.5%), and content freshness (average content age 393 days for winners vs 500 days for losers, a 107-day gap). - [Raptive](https://raptive.com/blog/what-googles-december-2025-core-update-tells-us-about-quality/)
- Google's ranking systems guide lists "Original content systems" (prioritising original reporting over pages merely citing it) and the "Reviews system" that rewards "insightful analysis and original research". - [Google ranking systems guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide)
- (older, May 2023) Google's news "topic authority" system weighs: notability for a topic or location (e.g., The Tennessean for Nashville high-school football), original reporting and influence ("the publisher that first broke a story" as cited by others), and source reputation (history of high-quality reporting, journalistic awards). It is designed to surface trusted regional publications above larger national outlets covering the same story. - [Google Search Central blog](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/05/understanding-news-topic-authority); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-launches-new-topic-authority-system-to-better-surface-news-content-427485)

### Inferences
- The strongest documented differentiator for a small site in 2025-2026 is owning assets competitors cannot copy. For snkrscart.com these already exist in the database: India retail prices and resale offers per size, seller listing counts, stock/sold-out history, drop dates in IST, and verification photos. Exposing these as structured, dated facts inside blog and guide pages is the direct application of information gain.
- Zyppy's "task completion" and "product or service" traits mean a sneaker blog attached to a working store (buy, set a drop reminder, add to calendar, check size availability) is structurally favoured over pure news sites like Sneaker News or Hypebeast, which the same study says lost the most.
- The topic authority model (notability for a location, original reporting first cited by others) suggests being the first and most-cited source for India-specific release facts (India launch dates, SNKRS India timings, Indian retailer raffles) is a realistic path to Top Stories for Indian queries.

### Gaps
- Nobody outside Google has shown that the information gain patent is live in production; all "information gain" evidence is correlational.
- No study isolates the effect of original photos specifically; "images" appear only as one example of proprietary assets in the Zyppy study.

## KQ5. AI Overviews and AI Mode (2025-2026): effect on clicks to informational blogs and which formats still earn clicks

### Takeaway
Measured CTR losses are large and growing: position one loses about 58% of clicks when an AI Overview appears (Ahrefs, Dec 2025, up from 34.5% in April 2025), Pew found organic clicks fall from 15% to 8% of visits with a summary present, and AI Mode sessions end without an external click 92-94% of the time (Semrush). AI Overviews trigger least on shopping, sports and news queries and most on long question-style queries, so release news, price/availability pages and tools are the formats least exposed.

### Cited Findings
- Pew Research (July 22, 2025): 900 US adults, March 2025, 68,879 searches, 12,593 (18%) produced an AI summary. With a summary, 8% of visits clicked a traditional result vs 15% without; only 1% of visits clicked a source cited inside the summary; 26% of visits with a summary ended the browsing session vs 16% without. Question-style searches produced summaries 60% of the time, 10+ word searches 53%, one-or-two-word searches 8%. 88% of summaries cited three or more sources. Wikipedia, YouTube and Reddit were the most cited; news sites were 5% of sources in both summaries and standard results. - [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)
- Google disputed the Pew methodology. - [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-disputes-pew-study-showing-ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-by-half/)
- Ahrefs (December 2025), 300,000 keywords (150,000 with AI Overview, 150,000 without): CTR change when an AI Overview appears: position 1 -58.0%, 2 -50.8%, 3 -46.4%, 4 -38.8%, 5 -32.6%, 6 -30.5%, 7 -29.7%, 8 -28.8%, 9 -29.7%, 10 -19.4%. Ahrefs' April 2025 study had found -34.5% for position one. "for every 100 clicks you could historically earn for a top-ranking page, Google now 'keeps' 58." About 9% of AI Overviews appear outside position one. - [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update)
- Semrush and Seer AI Mode study (August 2025): about 69 million US desktop searches, May 1 to July 5, 2025; 6-8% of AI Mode sessions led to a visit to an external domain, so 92-94% were zero-click; ordinary Google search zero-click rates were 35-46% depending on AI Overview presence. - [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-ai-mode-seo-impact/)
- AI Overview prevalence and intent mix (secondary summaries of Semrush/BrightEdge data): AI Overviews rose from 6.49% of US queries in January 2025 to about 25% in July 2025, then fell to 15.69% in November 2025; informational queries were 91.3% of AI Overview triggers in January 2025 and 57.1% by October 2025, with commercial around 19% and navigational up from 0.74% to 10.33%; only 13-14% of shopping keywords trigger an AI Overview; lowest-share sectors were Shopping (3.2%), Real Estate (5.8%), Sports (14.8%) and News (15.1%). - [ALM Corp summary of Semrush study](https://almcorp.com/blog/semrush-ai-overviews-study-2026-complete-analysis/); [Serps.io industry benchmarks](https://serps.io/blog/ai-overview-prevalence-by-industry); [ALM Corp on shopping queries](https://almcorp.com/blog/google-ai-overviews-shopping-queries/)
- Google's own documentation (2025-12-10) describes "query fan-out" ("issuing multiple related searches across subtopics and data sources") as how AI Mode and AI Overviews assemble answers and claims "when people click from search results pages with AI Overviews, these clicks are higher quality (meaning, users are more likely to spend more time on the site)". Existing preview controls (nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet, noindex) govern inclusion. - [Google Search Central, AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- Secondary aggregate claims (no primary methodology seen): publisher traffic from Google Search fell roughly 33% globally in 2025; zero-click on news queries rose from 56% to 69% in twelve months; LLM referrals are about 1% of publisher traffic; a preregistered August 2026 field experiment with 1,100 participants found AI Mode reduced external CTR by 18.8 percentage points. - [Digitaleer](https://www.digitaleer.com/ai-impact-google-search-traffic-2026/); [Fondure](https://fondure.com/2026/08/29/ai-search-publisher-traffic-2026/); [PPC Land on 39.8% publisher click cut](https://ppc.land/researchers-find-google-ai-overviews-cut-publisher-clicks-39-8/)
- Discover has become the dominant Google referrer for publishers: 68% of Google traffic to nearly 2,000 global news and media sites came from Discover by August 2025, with Search at 32% (secondary report of Chartbeat-type data). - [IMA Press](https://www.ima.press/post/google-discover-now-makes-up-two-thirds-of-search-traffic)
- AI Overviews reached over 2 billion monthly users across 200+ countries and 40 languages (Google figure, September 2025). - [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/09/223-google-ai-expands-to-5-new-languages/)

### Inferences
- Content formats with the lowest AI Overview exposure match this blog's strengths: shopping/price queries (3-14% trigger rate), sports and news (about 15%), short branded queries ("jordan 4 white cement india") which rarely trigger summaries. Long "what is / how to" guides are the most exposed format and should be written to earn a citation and a brand impression rather than for raw clicks.
- Because Google says AI Mode "fans out" into sub-queries, pages that answer narrow factual sub-questions with verifiable data (India price, release date in IST, retailer list, size run) are the ones most likely to be cited.
- Discover (two-thirds of publisher Google traffic by 2025) is now a larger opportunity than Search for news-style posts; see KQ6 for the February 2026 Discover update which favours in-country sites.

### Gaps
- All CTR studies are US-centric; no India-specific AI Overview trigger or CTR data was found.
- No study breaks out CTR impact for "release date" or "calendar" style queries specifically.

## KQ6. Google News, Top Stories and Discover eligibility for a small publisher

### Takeaway
There is no application, markup or minimum size to be eligible for Google News, Top Stories or Discover; Google says publishers are "automatically considered" if they comply with news content policies, and Discover needs only indexing plus 1200 px images with max-image-preview:large. The February 5, 2026 Discover core update explicitly favours "websites based in their country", "in-depth, original, and timely content" and topic-by-topic expertise, and will expand to all countries and languages.

### Cited Findings
- Google Publisher Center help: "Publishers are automatically considered for 'Top stories' or the News tab of Search." The only requirement is to "produce high-quality content and comply with Google News content policies." Top Stories: "Content is automatically selected for this feature." - [Google Publisher Center help](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/9607025)
- Secondary: since the Publisher Center transition completed in late March 2025 there is no manual submission or approval process for News-tab or Top Stories eligibility. - [SEO Handbook](https://seohandbook.co.uk/search-surfaces/news-seo-top-stories/); [Patrick Stox](https://patrickstox.com/technical-seo/news-seo/google-news-seo/)
- Article structured data doc (last updated 2026-09-08): "While there's no markup requirement to be eligible for Google News features like Top stories, you can add Article to more explicitly tell Google what your content is about." Recommended properties: author (Person or Organization with name and URL), datePublished and dateModified (ISO 8601 with timezone), headline, image. Images: multiple high-resolution images in 16x9, 4x3 and 1x1 aspect ratios, "minimum of 50K pixels when multiplying width and height", crawlable and indexable. Author best practice: include all visible authors, use author.name for the name only, add author type and URL/sameAs. - [Google Search Central, Article structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article)
- Discover doc (last updated 2026-03-09): "No special tags or structured data are required"; content must be indexed and meet Discover content policies. Images: at least 1200 px wide, over 300,000 total pixels, 16:9 recommended, enabled by `max-image-preview:large` or AMP, specified via schema.org or og:image; avoid logos and text-heavy images. Best practices: compelling titles that capture the essence, "avoid clickbait, sensationalism, and misleading preview details", timely or story-driven or unique-insight content, strong page experience. "Traffic from Discover is less predictable or dependable when compared to keyword-driven search visits." - [Google Search Central, Discover](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)
- February 2026 Discover core update, Google post by John Mueller, February 5, 2026 (verbatim): the update improves Discover by "Showing users more locally relevant content from websites based in their country", "Reducing sensational content and clickbait in Discover", and "Showing more in-depth, original, and timely content from websites with expertise in a given area". On expertise: "a local news site with a dedicated gardening section could have established expertise in gardening, even though it covers other topics. In contrast, a movie review site that wrote a single article about gardening would likely not." Rollout began for US English users and will expand to "all countries and languages in the months ahead"; Google points creators to the core updates guidance and the Discover "how content appears" help page. - [Google Search Central blog](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-releases-discover-core-update-february-2026-468308)
- Status dashboard: the February 2026 Discover update started February 5, 2026 and lasted 21 days 17 hours (completion around February 27, 2026). - [Google Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history)
- Discover is available in seven additional Indian languages (Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Kannada, Punjabi, Bengali, Malayalam), collectively over 700 million speakers (Marfeel product note; date not stated). - [Marfeel](https://www.marfeel.com/whatsnew/google-discover-now-available-in-7-major-indian-languages)
- Indian publisher guidance on the Discover update (SEO-community advice, not Google): set Search Console country targeting to India, use fact-first non-clickbait headlines, build topic-level expertise, 1200 px images, lower ad density, prefer first-hand reporting over aggregation. - [The Publive](https://www.thepublive.com/blog/google/the-local-first-era-what-googles-february-2026-discover-core-update-means-for-publishers-11099179)
- Google's ranking systems guide lists "Local news systems" that surface local sources in "Top stories" and "Local news". - [Google ranking systems guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide)

### Inferences
- The February 2026 Discover update is the single most favourable Google change for an India-based sneaker publisher: once it reaches India, an in-country site with a consistent sneaker section should be preferred over US-based Sneaker News or Hypebeast for Indian Discover users, provided the section shows sustained topical depth and avoids clickbait titles.
- Minimum technical checklist for Top Stories and Discover on snkrscart.com blog pages: NewsArticle or Article JSON-LD with author (Person with URL to an author page), datePublished and dateModified in ISO 8601 with +05:30, headline, publisher logo, images in 16:9, 4:3 and 1:1 at 1200 px+ width, `<meta name="robots" content="max-image-preview:large">`, and og:image set to the hero image. None of these are required by Google, but every one is "recommended" in current docs.

### Gaps
- Google has not published a rollout date for the Discover update in India or in Hindi.
- No Google documentation specifies a minimum publishing frequency for Top Stories; frequency claims in SEO blogs are inference.

## KQ7. Freshness: how release-date and price pages benefit from updates, dateModified and "query deserves freshness"

### Takeaway
Google documents "Freshness systems" that show newer content when timeliness matters, and tells publishers to show a visible date plus datePublished/dateModified that match, while John Mueller has repeatedly said changing dates without substantive changes does nothing. The only measured 2025-2026 freshness effect found is Raptive's: December 2025 core update winners averaged 393-day-old content vs 500 days for losers.

### Cited Findings
- Google ranking systems guide: "Freshness systems" show newer content for queries where timeliness matters, such as recent movie reviews or breaking news (paraphrase of Google description). - [Google ranking systems guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide)
- Publication dates doc (last updated 2025-12-10): show a clear user-visible date ("Posted", "Published", "Last updated"); "Add a subtype of CreativeWork (such as Article, BlogPosting, or VideoObject), and specify the datePublished and/or dateModified fields"; "Ensure that the date (and optional time and timezone) match between the equivalent user-visible and structured values"; "Don't specify future dates, or the date of the action described on the page"; "Minimize the presence of other dates on the page"; "Google doesn't depend on a single date factor because all factors can be prone to issues. That's why our systems look at several factors to determine our best estimate of when a page was published or significantly updated." - [Google Search Central, publication dates](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/publication-dates)
- John Mueller: "an update to the main content, the structured data, or links on the page is generally considered significant, however an update to the copyright date is not"; automatically setting current dates in XML sitemaps provides no ranking benefit. - [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-john-mueller-updating-xml-sitemap-dates-doesnt-help-seo/545547/)
- (older, 2021) John Mueller: changing dates on pages won't improve rankings. - [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-john-mueller-changing-dates-on-pages-wont-improve-rankings/401734/)
- Raptive (Feb 2026): December 2025 core update winners had average content age of 393 days vs 500 days for losers, a 107-day gap, attributed to "meaningful content updates focused on quality". - [Raptive](https://raptive.com/blog/what-googles-december-2025-core-update-tells-us-about-quality/)
- Ahrefs lists "content refreshes with current information" as one way to add information gain. - [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/information-gain/)
- Google's February 2026 Discover update explicitly rewards "timely" content alongside "in-depth" and "original". - [Google Search Central blog](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update)

### Inferences
- A release-date page legitimately changes state (rumoured, confirmed, raffle open, released, restocked, sold out, price change). Each state change is a substantive main-content update, which is exactly the case where Google says a fresh dateModified is appropriate. Logging the change inline (an "Updates" block with timestamps) satisfies both the visible-date and structured-date rules and is itself original data.
- Price pages should update dateModified only when the price or availability actually changes, not on every build/deploy; the current frontend uses ISR/revalidation, so dateModified must come from the data change time, not render time.

### Gaps
- "Query deserves freshness" (QDF) is not documented anywhere in current Google Search Central material; the term dates from a 2007 New York Times report and no 2025-2026 Google source was found that confirms or names it.
- The often-quoted Google line "don't artificially freshen a story without adding significant information" could not be located in any current Google page during this research; treat it as unverified (it was formerly in Google News help).
- No controlled 2025-2026 experiment on dateModified and ranking was found.

## KQ9. Core updates in 2025 and 2026: dates, winners and losers, patterns relevant to product and commerce blogs

### Takeaway
Google ran core updates in March, June and December 2025 and March and May 2026, plus a Discover-only core update in February 2026 and five spam updates. The December 2025 update (the year's most volatile) rewarded e-commerce and specialist retailers and punished UGC platforms, health and news publishers; 2026 analyses (Zyppy, Amsive) show informational publishers and aggregators continuing to lose to sites with products, tasks and proprietary assets, while only about 22% of September-2023 HCU-hit sites have shown any meaningful recovery.

### Cited Findings
- Google Search Status Dashboard entries: March 2025 core update (started Mar 13, 2025, 13 days 21 hours); June 2025 core update (Jun 30, 2025, 16 days 18 hours); August 2025 spam update (Aug 26, 2025, 26 days 15 hours); December 2025 core update (Dec 11, 2025, 18 days 2 hours); February 2026 Discover update (Feb 5, 2026, 21 days 17 hours); March 2026 spam update (Mar 24, 2026, 19 h 30 min); March 2026 core update (Mar 27, 2026, 12 days 4 hours); May 2026 core update (May 21, 2026, 11 days 21 hours); June 2026 spam update (Jun 24, 2026, 2 days 1 hour); August 2026 spam update (Aug 18, 2026, 2 days 16 hours); September 2026 spam update (started Sep 24, 2026). - [Google Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history)
- May 2026 core update ran May 21, 2026 8:40 AM PDT to June 2, 2026 5:40 AM PDT; Google said it aims to "better surface relevant, satisfying content for searchers from all types of sites" with "nothing new or special that creators need to do"; mobile volatility was higher than desktop; early winners gave back part of their gains late in the rollout. - [SEO Kreativ](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/google-may-2026-core-update-started/); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-may-2026-core-update-rollout-is-now-complete-479119)
- March 2026 core update ran March 27 to April 8, 2026; Amsive data showed it pulled visibility away from aggregators and UGC platforms. - [SEO Kreativ](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/google-march-2026-core-update-complete/); [TechWyse on Amsive data](https://www.techwyse.com/news/industry-news/google-march-2026-core-update-aggregators-ugc-visibility-loss)
- December 2025 core update (Dec 11 to Dec 29, 2025), Amsive analysis of Sistrix US Visibility Index across 803 domains: winners led by e-commerce/apparel (Aritzia +56%, JCPenney +52%, The North Face +52%), finance (+15% average), curated review sites (Trustpilot +49%, Thesaurus.com +41.9 points/+33%) and travel (Vacasa +79%); losers included UGC platforms (Wikipedia -435 points/-5%, Facebook -7%, Reddit -82.85 points/-3%, Quora -25%), health publishers (Healthline -27%, Medical News Today -42%, WebMD -19%), news (NY Times -21%, Reuters -34%, Newsweek -59%) and reference sites (Merriam-Webster -11%, Britannica -16%). Pattern: "Major user-generated content platforms experienced sweeping visibility losses"; moderated-review sites gained; some social platforms rebounded post-update. - [Amsive](https://www.amsive.com/insights/seo/googles-december-2025-core-update-winners-losers-analysis/)
- Secondary analyses of the same update report that fashion specialists (Shein, Boohoo, River Island) gained 38-120% while generalist retailers (H&M, ASOS, Marks & Spencer) lost 7.3-27.2%, and that the update produced the highest volatility of 2025; these figures were not verified against a primary dataset. - [SEOZoom (Italy)](https://www.seozoom.com/december-2025-update-analysis/); [Seoptimer](https://www.seoptimer.com/blog/december-2025-core-update/)
- HCU recovery: Glenn Gabe tracked about 400 sites "obliterated" by the September 2023 helpful content update (40-90% traffic loss); only 88 (22%) showed a 20%+ lift and almost none returned to baseline; Lily Ray's analysis of the 130 hardest-hit sites found 129 had only declined since September 2023 (as summarised by a secondary source). - [The STACC](https://thestacc.com/blog/helpful-content-update-recovery-study/); [SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-news-recovery-after-hcu)
- Zyppy (Cyrus Shepard), 400 sites through March 2026: "informational sites (news publishers and affiliate sites) lost the most traffic"; winners "offer something that AI cannot easily replicate". - [Practical Ecommerce](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/organic-search-winners-share-5-traits)
- Raptive on December 2025: winners had faster LCP, under 25% ad density, fewer thin pages, higher branded search share and fresher content (see KQ4 for numbers). - [Raptive](https://raptive.com/blog/what-googles-december-2025-core-update-tells-us-about-quality/)
- Marie Haynes documented four sites that recovered from the December 2025 core update and what they changed (secondary listing; details not retrieved). - [Search Engine Journal via TheATDB](https://www.theatdb.com/news/4-sites-that-recovered-from-googles-december-2025-core-update-what-they-changed-via-sejournal-mariehaynes)

### Inferences
- The two-year pattern is consistent: Google is shifting visibility from pure informational publishers and aggregators toward sites that sell, enable a task, and own data. A sneaker blog that is part of a store and exposes store data has the winning profile; the pure sneaker news sites it competes with have the losing profile.
- The "specialist beats generalist" retail pattern in December 2025 (if accurate) supports keeping the blog tightly focused on the five store brands rather than broadening into lifestyle or streetwear generally.
- Recovery data (22% of HCU-hit sites recovering) means a site-level quality demotion is very hard to reverse; it is far cheaper to avoid thin AI release posts than to recover from a classifier hit.

### Gaps
- Google never publishes winners/losers; all sector data above comes from third-party visibility indices (Sistrix, Semrush) on US or European markets. No India-market core update analysis was found.
- No Google statement explains what changed in any individual 2025-2026 core update beyond the generic "better surface relevant, satisfying content" language.
