# Skip named-store comparisons, ship dated price ranges

**Do not build the named-store comparison now; extend the anonymous market range the store already ships, and treat named stores as a gated later phase.** Judged on the owner's main goal (ranking in Google India and reaching more Indian users), the named version fails first on evidence, before risk is even counted. India autocomplete supports "<model> price in india" for all 10 models tested, but the comparison-shaped queries are thin: the store's own Search Console shows **no query containing "price comparison", "compare price" or "cheapest"**, and the 37 cheapest/lowest/deal queries it does appear for earned **63 impressions and 0 clicks** in 90 days (store's own audit, 2026-11-21). The data cannot yet support the page either: **no scraped row from any of nine sources carries a manufacturer style code**, so the same pair cannot be proven identical across stores, and **no price history is stored**. The risk side points the same way: six named stores forbid scraping in their written terms, two of them forbid "collection and use of product listings, description, or prices", a Merchant Center account reinstated only on 2026-10-20 sits on the same domain, and **54 of 85 benchmarked items are more than 5 percent above Google's India benchmark**, so the store would usually be told to buy elsewhere on its own pages. The recommended path is four phases: measure the shipped price pages and start storing history, capture style codes for a 30 to 50 model shortlist, upgrade the anonymous range with dated rows and alerts, and only then ask named stores for written permission. Section 4 lists the conditions that would make the named version worth building.

## Comparison pages answer a query almost nobody types

The demand that exists is for price, not for comparison. Google India autocomplete (queried directly with an India IP) returned the exact phrase "<model> price in india" for **all 10 models tested**, including long-tail colour and retailer variants such as "flipkart" and "amazon". The comparison shapes fall away fast: "price comparison" appeared for 7 of 10 models, "where to buy <model> in india" for 2 of 10, and "sneaker price tracker india", "compare sneaker prices india" and the Hinglish shapes returned empty lists. Autocomplete shows existence, never volume, so an empty list means very low or no volume, and the "cheapest <model>" results carried UK, Philippines and South Africa signals rather than Indian ones (research notes, India autocomplete test, 2026-11-21).

The store's own Search Console agrees. Across 2,173 queries, 24,324 impressions and 358 clicks in 90 days, queries containing "price in india" made up 35 queries and 369 impressions (1.5 percent) at average position 16.8, with 2 clicks. Every "vs" query was brand against brand ("nike vs adidas which is better") or country against country, none store against store, and the single store-name query that matters ("nike mind 001 myntra", 53 impressions, 3 clicks, position 3.3) is a shoe-plus-store query that the store already almost wins without a comparison page (store's own audit). The export understates market demand, since it shows only queries the site already appears for, but it shows which query shapes Google already associates with the site, and comparison is not one of them. A named page would be matched mostly by "<store A> vs <store B>" and "is <store> legit" queries, which are about the stores, not the shoes, and whose autocomplete variants are dominated by "reddit" intent (research notes, demand pass).

The wider evidence on comparison content is unfavourable to a new domain, though none of it is India-specific. An Ahrefs study found comparison keywords were the largest category of keywords lost (2,555, or 41 percent) by seven big affiliate publishers after the September 2023 helpful content update ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/affiliate-publisher-site-reputation-abuse/)). After the March 2026 core update, aggregators and platforms lost visibility while sites that "own the thing" gained ([Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/googles-march-core-update-shifted-visibility-away-from-aggregators/573621)). Even the largest German comparison brand, idealo, moved about 13 to 20 percent in two weeks of November 2026 ([Dr. Web](https://www.drweb.de/core-update-preisvergleiche-sichtbarkeit/)). No source was found of a domain under one year old ranking a comparison section, and the store's domain dates from April 2026. Google's own written guidance lists "product comparisons" as the good alternative to thin affiliation, but also names scraping ("republishing content from other sites without adding any original content or value") and doorway pages ("substantially similar pages" built for similar queries) as spam, and Mueller once described showing merchant feeds side by side as "not really adding value, you are just like showing two affiliate feeds" ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies); [SERoundtable, 2017](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-two-affiliate-feeds-value-23807.html)). Nine stores times a catalogue of roughly 120 products is exactly the template-at-scale shape those lines describe unless each page carries data nobody else has.

Google is also building the answer itself. On 7 April 2026 it announced AI shopping in India across the Gemini app, AI Mode and Circle to Search, with comparison tables of price, reviews and inventory drawn from a Shopping Graph of over 50 billion products ([Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/explore-communicate/new-ways-google-is-using-ai-to-make-shopping-easier/)). That graph is fed by merchant feeds, so for a "price in india" query the store's best route into AI answers is a healthy Merchant Center feed, not a third-party comparison page. I could not observe an India results page, so how that panel looks for sneaker queries is unverified. On the other side of the ledger, no sneaker-specific, same-shoe, cross-store comparison for Indian specialist stores turned up in any search, and the Indian price-history tools found (PriceHistory, Flipshope, BuyHatke) did not list VegNonVeg, Superkicks or Culture Circle ([PriceHistory](https://pricehistoryapp.com/deals/footwear)). That is a real gap, but a gap with no visible search demand for the tool itself is not an opening to bet the domain on.

On traffic arithmetic, the affiliate route does not rescue the idea. Cookies are short (Myntra 1 day, Ajio 1 day, Tata CLiQ 7 days), footwear rates are gated behind sign-up, and Culture Circle, Crepdog Crew, Mainstreet, HypeFly and Limited Edt showed no programme at all, so most named stores would receive visitors and pay nothing ([Cuelinks Myntra](https://www.cuelinks.com/campaigns/myntra-affiliate-program); [Cuelinks Ajio](https://www.cuelinks.com/campaigns/ajio-affiliate-program); [Cuelinks Tata CLiQ](https://www.cuelinks.com/campaigns/tata-cliq-affiliate-program)). At a 4.5 to 5.4 percent headline rate on a Rs 10,000 order, Rs 10,000 a month needs roughly 19 to 22 validated orders, which under stated assumptions means 1,300 to 7,300 qualified visits a month, against a store whose Search Console total was 874 clicks in October 2026, 587 of them from India (store's own November report). The query export counts only 358 clicks in 90 days because it is a subset of that total: Search Console leaves rare queries out of query-level rows. Either way, the payoff needs a multiple of current traffic and arrives after 60 to 90 days of validation. Those conversion assumptions are illustrative, since no Indian click-out rate was found.

## The data cannot yet prove two listings are the same shoe

The store's scraper holds **4,100 listings across nine sources, of which 2,370 are priced, adult and seen in the last 30 days**, but the structure blocks the product being proposed (store's own audit). No source stores a manufacturer style code as a field; the `sku` column holds each retailer's internal id (an Ajio id such as `469811286002`, a Foot Locker id such as `NIKEX00089031`). Of 1,839 distinct `sku` values, 1,838 appear on exactly one store, and the single exception joins Tata CLiQ to Tata CLiQ Luxury, which are one company. The store's own catalogue has 90 shoe products with 89 real style codes, and **zero of those codes appear anywhere in a scraped row** (sku, URL, name or description). Loose name matching links only 14 of 90 products to at least one store and 3 to three stores, and it is the same model-level logic that on 2026-10-10 linked 44 drops to products and got the colourway wrong on 43. A page that says "same pair, Rs 800 cheaper at store X" cannot stand on that. When it is wrong, it is a false statement about a named rival, which is the exposure Indian courts police.

The overlap is thin by nature as well. Ajio and Tata CLiQ rows are mostly mass-market pairs (Court Vision Low at Rs 3,248, Downshifter 14 at Rs 3,916), while the store's stock is Jordan 1, Jordan 4, Dunk and New Balance lifestyle pairs at roughly Rs 8,000 to 20,000. Per-size prices are absent for most sources: sizes exist for Superkicks, Foot Locker, Limited Edt and the stale Myntra feed, but none for Ajio, Tata CLiQ, Tata CLiQ Luxury or VegNonVeg, and no source gives a price per size. Myntra has not scraped successfully since 2026-10-08 (zero rows seen in 30 days), Nike India has no rows, and Culture Circle, Crepdog Crew, Mainstreet, Dawntown and HypeFly are not scraped at all, even though Culture Circle is the first store the owner wants to name. Foot Locker rows include clothing. Colourway is stored only for Tata CLiQ rows.

Closing that gap is real engineering with real cost. The style code is printed on VegNonVeg, Superkicks, Limited Edt and Foot Locker product pages but not in listing JSON, so matching needs one extra request per product per store, on hosts that already rate-limit (Superkicks answered a products.json probe with HTTP 429 and a 60 second retry) or block (Foot Locker's Akamai blocks an IP after heavy probing, Ajio needs a headless browser, a paid ScrapingAnt fallback is metered). New scrapers would be needed for Culture Circle, Crepdog Crew and Mainstreet, plus a price-history collection, a style-code match table, freshness rules and a per-store takedown switch. The backend is on Render's free tier and the frontend on Vercel Hobby, which paused the project on 2026-11-18 for CPU and ISR writes, and pages whose content changes twice a day add ISR writes. A named-store build is a multi-week data project before it is a page.

## Policy, law and store terms stack up against naming rivals

Google's written rules do not ban a merchant from showing competitor prices, but they give no safe harbour, and the sharpest exposure is Merchant Center rather than Search. Merchant listing eligibility requires that "the merchant has to be the seller", and pages that only link to other sellers are excluded; structured data may use AggregateOffer for a product sold by several merchants, but it must match what readers can see, and a scraper run that changes a price after the JSON-LD was written produces exactly the mismatch the policies punish ([Merchant listing](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing); [Product snippet](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product-snippet); [Structured data policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies)). Merchant Center's landing page rules say "Don't redirect people to another website outside of the domain you claimed", and Google says it reviews "your listing, website, accounts, and third-party sources", warns that accounts are "only reinstated in compelling circumstances" and may ban repeat violators ([Landing page requirements](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/4752265); [Policy enforcement](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6150127)). The store was suspended for misrepresentation in October 2026, one cause being a promise (free shipping) that checkout contradicted, and reinstated on 2026-10-20 with 405 of 407 items eligible. No Google text addresses competitor prices on a merchant's own domain, so the risk is my inference from the whole-site review language, but a second suspension is the single costliest outcome in this plan and no evidence shows a repeat is easier to appeal. Google's CSS programme does not cover India, so there is no sanctioned comparison route.

Indian law leaves price facts lightly protected but the contract and accuracy exposure real. No Indian statute or reported judgment decides whether scraping public price data is unlawful; individual prices are facts, while wholesale copying of a curated database has been restrained (Info Edge v Bixee, 2005; Just Dial) ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/economy-policy/hc-tells-site-not-to-use-naukri-com-content-105122901007_1.html); [Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/delhi-high-court-grants-relief-to-just-dial-in-suit-alleging-data-theft-trademark-violation)). Whether browsewrap "no scraping" terms bind a non-registered visitor is unsettled ([law.asia](https://law.asia/india-data-scraping-regulation/)). Comparative advertising must be true and not disparaging, and the 2025 Dabur v Patanjali rulings turned on specific and generic disparagement ([Legal Service India](https://www.legalserviceindia.com/Legal-Articles/?p=11573)). A "cheapest at X" line is a specific factual claim about a named rival; if X's price moved after the scrape, it was false when the customer read it. The CCPA misleading-advertisement guidelines of 2022 carry penalties up to Rs 10 lakh for a first offence and Rs 50 lakh after, and the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Amendment Rules, 2026 take effect on 1 January 2027, barring manipulation of rankings (Rule 4(11)(c)) and requiring distinct labelling of sponsored listings (Rule 4(12)), which makes an unlabelled "recommended store" ordering that favours the store's own row a fresh compliance question ([Khaitan Ergo](https://khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2022-06/Ergo_11062022.pdf); [Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026-from-consumer-disclosure-to-platform-governance)). I found no Indian case on stale price displays or on one retailer naming another's prices, and the dark-pattern annexure and the guidelines' comparative-advertising clause were not read, so a lawyer should review before any launch.

The named stores' own words are the hardest fact. Of twelve hosts checked on 2026-11-21, six forbid scraping or automated access in their terms: Myntra, VegNonVeg, Superkicks, Crepdog Crew, Culture Circle and Mainstreet. VegNonVeg and Superkicks go further and exclude "collection and use of product listings, description, or prices", Culture Circle bars "data mining, robots, or similar data gathering or extraction tools", and VegNonVeg, Culture Circle and Limited Edt reserve logos, images or pricing ([VegNonVeg](https://www.vegnonveg.com/legal); [Superkicks](https://www.superkicks.in/pages/terms-conditions); [Culture Circle](https://www.culture-circle.com/terms-conditions); [Limited Edt](https://limitededt.in/pages/terms-conditions)). Foot Locker India, Ajio and nike.in returned Akamai 403 to the research fetches, and Tata CLiQ's terms could not be read. The store's scraper already touches all of these quietly, so today the exposure is private; publishing their names, prices and a "buy there instead" verdict turns it into something a direct competitor in the same Delhi and Gurugram niche can see and act on, most plausibly with a legal notice or a technical block. Where the scraper uses workarounds against a 403, the owner also hands a rival an IT Act s.43 argument. Culture Circle adds a specific trap: it is not scraped, its terms ban extraction, and a new scraper would be a deliberate breach of the very site the owner most wants to feature. Logos and competitor photos carry moderate to high risk, and text names with a link the lowest.

Then there is the business effect. A comparison run by a seller that loses most comparisons trains visitors to leave. With 54 of 85 benchmarked items above Google's benchmark, the page would usually name someone else as cheaper, on a domain with 2 failed-payment orders to its name. The Progressive precedent (showing rival quotes even when higher) is a 2005 commentary with no results data and fits a firm that is competitive on a large share of quotes; academic work finds comparison-site adopters cut shopping at branded retailers by about a tenth ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/progressive-insurance-quotes-competitors-rates/); [NBER w9942](https://nber.org/papers/w9942)). Indian buyers also weigh authenticity above price: Culture Circle's founder calls counterfeits the greatest concern, and resellers argue import duty legitimately inflates Indian prices ([DQ India](https://www.dqindia.com/interview/we-are-creating-a-truly-international-standard-of-verification-culture-circle-9083308); [Hustle Culture](https://hustleculture.co.in/blogs/articles/is-buying-premium-sneakers-from-india-based-stores-safe)). No study here gives the price gap that makes a buyer switch, so whether a Rs 500 gap outweighs trust for a new store is judgement, not measurement.

## Named stores versus anonymous range, and the phased verdict

The anonymous range already ships: sneaker profile pages titled "<Model> Price in India" with a dated answer sentence, lowest, middle and highest price, a listing count, and a table of the store's own pairs, plus the `/sneakers/price-in-india` hub, built 2026-11-21 and not yet measured. It uses model-level matching, which is honest for a range because the page says "model" and shows a spread, but it could never support a same-pair claim. The two variants compare as follows.

| Question                          | Named-store comparison                                                                        | Anonymous range plus own offers                                          |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Matches the query people type     | Weak: comparison shapes thin, store-vs-store queries are about stores                         | Strong: "price in india" present for 10 of 10 models                     |
| Needs same-pair proof             | Yes, and the data has none (0 style codes in scraped rows)                                    | No, a labelled model-level spread is enough                              |
| Google value test                 | Close to "two feeds side by side" unless history, sizes and style code are added              | Own data, dated, plus own stock, closer to "owns the thing"              |
| Merchant Center exposure          | Highest, on a domain reinstated 2026-10-20                                                    | Low, no competitor names or outbound links                               |
| Store terms and legal             | Six named stores forbid scraping, two forbid price collection; stale price equals false claim | Facts aggregated, no names, no logos, no links                           |
| Effect when store is not cheapest | Page tells buyers to leave (54 of 85 items above benchmark)                                   | Own price shown against a range, honest label such as "near the low end" |
| Build cost                        | Style-code capture, three new scrapers, history, matching, freshness, takedown switch         | History collection and polish on what exists                             |
| Reversibility                     | Naming rivals cannot be quietly undone                                                        | Can add names later once permitted                                       |

The verdict is no to the named version now, yes to a stronger anonymous version, and the plan is phased so each step earns the next. In phase 0 (now, about four weeks), do nothing public: let the shipped price pages collect eight weeks of Search Console data, apply for the Google Trends API alpha, build the shortlist in section 5, and add a price-history collection so every day of delay is not another lost day of data; the ingest route currently overwrites each listing in place, so history starts only the day it is added. In phase 1, add to the anonymous pages what Google names as value and competitors lack: a 30, 90 and all-time history toggle (the one chart pattern both PriceRunner and Smartprix share), per-size availability where the scraper has sizes, the style code for the store's own pairs, a visible "checked" date, a hide-after-48-hours rule so stale rows drop out of the range, and a price-drop alert asking for size and target price. In phase 2, capture style codes only for the shortlist of 30 to 50 models from the stores that print them and allow it, respecting robots.txt (Nike disallows `*/p/` product paths; Superkicks, VegNonVeg and Tata CLiQ disallow search) and stopping on a 403 instead of working around it; use the codes to raise the range from model level to verified pair level, still unnamed. In phase 3, write to the stores that matter (VegNonVeg, Superkicks, Culture Circle, Crepdog) asking for written permission or a feed, and name only those that agree, with text names and links rather than logos or images, starting with a hand-reviewed pilot of about 10 models on guide URLs kept out of the Merchant feed.

The named version becomes worth building when most of the following hold, with thresholds being my judgement rather than sourced figures. At least two stores have granted written permission or a feed, so the contract risk is gone for those names. Style-code matching is verified by hand on the shortlist, with every named row backed by a code match and zero known colourway errors. At least eight weeks of stored history exist, so the page has data no one else has. The store is at or below the median of the comparison set on more than half the shortlisted models, a sharp contrast with today's 54 of 85 above benchmark, or the owner explicitly accepts sending buyers away as an acquisition play. The shipped price pages show traction (for example rising impressions and average positions inside the top 10 for several "price in india" queries), proving the domain can rank this content at all. Merchant Center has stayed clean for several months, a lawyer has reviewed the rival-naming wording and the 1 January 2027 e-commerce rules, and a visible takedown contact with a per-store off switch exists. If a source of demand appears (for instance Search Console showing "<model> vs" or "price comparison" queries for shoes), that also moves the case. Until then, the existing shopper-supplied "Found it cheaper? Verify the deal" form is the safe way to engage with rival prices, because the shopper, not the store, supplies the claim.

## Build the shortlist from five free signal families

The brief asked for free routes to find what is trending, in demand or newly launched in India. The honest answer is that no single free source gives India sneaker demand, so the store should fuse several, and the strongest ones are the store's own. Nothing here returns search volume, only rank or presence; the research environment's web search was US-only, so it could not show an India results page, which means manual checks should be done from an Indian phone or browser.

The first family is the store's own Search Console and Google autocomplete, which already beat every paid-looking alternative for India. Autocomplete with `gl=in&hl=en-IN` at `suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search` worked from an India IP and returned the price-in-india phrase for all 10 test models; it is free, scriptable and confirms which models attract Indian price queries, though a suggestion proves some searches exist, not how many. The second family is Merchant Center, where the store is reinstated and eligible: the Popular products report covers 36 countries including India, ranks products weekly or monthly with rank change, price range and availability, and exports CSV, and best sellers is the same demand rank, so the two count once; reports need a standalone or sub-account and the PERFORMANCE_REPORTING or ADMIN right, and weekly data takes up to two weeks to appear ([Popular products help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/9488679); [Merchant API reports](https://developers.google.com/merchant/api/guides/reports/overview)). Price competitiveness and price insights from the same account show where the store is above benchmark, which decides what to reprice, not what to add.

The third family is Google Trends. The official API is an application-only alpha launched 2025-07-24 with rolling five-year data and cross-country comparison, no stated quota, and no Trending Now ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/07/trends-api); [apply page](https://developers.google.com/search/apis/trends)), so apply for it but plan without it. The pytrends library was archived on 2025-04-17 and vendor reports on unofficial access conflict, so use the Trends website manually with an anchor method: pick one steady India shoe, query batches of four models plus the anchor with geo IN and a 12-month window, divide each by the anchor, and treat values under about 5 as noise. The fourth family is launch buzz: Sneaker News publishes a valid RSS feed with items dated 2026-11-20 (global, only seven items per fetch), and its model names can be matched against the store's own drop calendar, since India release dates must come from brand India pages and that calendar, not the feed ([Sneaker News feed](https://sneakernews.com/feed/)). The fifth is stores' new arrivals from allowed pages only: Superkicks and Limited Edt disallow `sort_by` collection URLs, so a "best-selling" sort is off limits, and default order or `published_at` is the usable signal, with terms still to be read. Reddit's free tier is non-commercial and its status is unclear, Instagram hashtag limits could not be verified, and Amazon India's best-seller paths are open in robots.txt but its terms were not read, so skip all three or read them by hand.

To combine them, normalise every model name first (style code, then brand plus model plus colourway tokens), then fuse ranks with reciprocal rank fusion. This recipe is standard practice, not sourced, and the weights are guesses: score each family f as w_f divided by (60 plus rank_f), sum across families, and multiply launch items by exp(minus days since signal divided by 30). Suggested weights are 0.35 Merchant, 0.25 own Search Console and autocomplete, 0.15 Trends anchor ratio, 0.15 launch buzz, 0.10 store new arrivals. Admit a model only when it appears in at least two independent families, or sits in the Merchant top 50 alone. For a 50-model list, keep the top 35 by score, reserve 10 slots for launches in the next 30 days and 5 for risers outside the top 35, then recalibrate the weights against the store's own sales after eight weeks. Add one filter specific to this project: keep a model only if at least two independent stores list the same pair, or the store stocks it, because a page without that overlap is the thin template Google penalises. For a worked example, a model at Merchant rank 20, Search Console rank 10 and launch-buzz rank 8 scores about 0.0102 before decay and gets in; a model at Merchant rank 5 alone scores 0.0054 and gets in only through the top-50 rule.

## On a phone: what is verified, what is design reasoning

Competitor layouts could not be opened. StockX's product page returned a shell with "Loading..." placeholders, Culture Circle's UAE page showed no size selector or offer table in captured text, the Internet Archive refused every fetch, and idealo, PriceRunner, 91mobiles, Smartprix, Google Shopping, GOAT, Klekt and Crepdog's size controls could not be read. **No design below is copied from a verified live competitor page.** What is verified is a short list of standards and feature-level facts, and everything else is my reasoning. Verified items are the Sneakers123 listing controls (a filter button, a sort menu of relevance, newest, highest price and lowest price, a size filter with a half-size toggle, a long editorial block after the results, and a footer stating it holds no stock and shows prices and returns before purchase) ([Sneakers123](https://www.sneakers123.com/en/sneaker/nike-air-force-1-07-cw2288-111)); NN/G's mobile table rules of sticky headers, a locked first column, visible cues when content scrolls sideways, legibility without zoom, and a narrow phone fitting only about two wordy columns ([NN/G](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tables/)); Baymard's sticky name, thumbnail and price, "only show differences" option and row separators ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/user-friendly-comparison-tools)); Google's pagination rules, which require real `<a href>` links and unique `?page=n` URLs, because load-more buttons alone hide products from Google ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/pagination-and-incremental-page-loading)); Smartprix's price history toggle of one month to whole life ([Smartprix](https://www.smartprix.com/bytes/smartprix-product-price-history-graph-unlocking-best-deals-and-avoiding-overpayment/)); the UK CMA's CARE principles (explain how you earn money, how you rank, how commercial relationships affected results, how much of the market you cover) ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/digital-comparison-tools-summary-of-final-report/digital-comparison-tools-summary-of-final-report)); WCAG 2.2 target size of at least 24 by 24 CSS px, GOV.UK table markup with caption and `scope`, web.dev's 0.1 layout shift target ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html); [GOV.UK table](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/table/); [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/cls)); and WhatsApp's rule that messaging needs explicit opt-in ([WhatsApp Business](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)). Google's price insights (labels of lower, typical or higher than normal, history, a 30-day-low flag computed on total price) is documented only in US-scoped help text, and KBB's fair market range with a midpoint and a "this week" window comes from search summaries ([Google Help](https://support.google.com/faqs/answer/10675605)).

Indian conventions (lakh grouping via en-IN formatting, "delivery by" after a pincode check, COD yes or no, MRP and country-of-origin lines under the Legal Metrology rules, a WhatsApp share button) are reasoning only; the Myntra, Ajio, Flipkart and Amazon India price lines were never viewed. The one verified Indian rule is that Legal Metrology Rule 6(10) requires mandatory declarations on e-commerce listings, so the store's own row should carry them ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/02/21/legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-amendment-rules-2026-explained/)). Before any build, a designer should open PriceRunner, idealo, 91mobiles, Smartprix, StockX, GOAT, Culture Circle India and Crepdog on a 360 px Android phone and screenshot them, since that is the single biggest verification gap.

## Conclusion

The idea fails less on legality than on arithmetic and sequence. Even if every legal risk were cleared, a named page needs matched pairs, price history and stores' goodwill, and the store has none of the three today: no style codes in any scraped row, no stored history, and permission from no one. The idea also pits the store against its own price position, since a page that works only when the store is cheapest will mostly say it is not. What the evidence does support is narrower and cheaper: Indians search "<model> price in india", the store has already built the answer, and Google's own policy language rewards pages that add data nobody else holds. History, per-size availability, style codes and a freshness rule are those data, and every one of them is also what a later named version would need, so the anonymous path loses nothing if permission arrives.

Two things remain unknown and decide the next move. First, nobody has seen an India results page for these queries, so whether Google's Shopping Graph answers "price in india" before any page does is unobserved; the owner's own Search Console, in eight weeks, will settle it better than any further desk research. Second, whether store permission is obtainable is untested: a short written request to four stores costs nothing and converts the largest legal and policy unknown into a known answer. Run both before writing any code for named rows.

## Addendum: SoleSpread (checked 2026-11-21)

The owner pointed to [SoleSpread](https://www.solespread.in/) after the report was written. Its public pages were read directly on 2026-11-21. It corrects one finding above: an Indian cross-store sneaker price tool does exist, and it names stores.

What it is, from its own pages: a "sneaker resale profit calculator for India" aimed at resellers, not shoppers. A search checks four named resale stores live (RIF LA in the USA, Kick Game in the UK, Mainstreet Marketplace and Crep Dog Crew in India), matches the same sneaker by style code, lines sizes up by UK size, converts at the day's exchange rate, adds shipping, customs duty and IGST to get a landed cost, compares that with the lowest in-stock Indian ask and gives a 0 to 100 score with a BUY, HOLD or EXIT call. Prices come "straight from the stores' own product pages", each page shows when it was fetched, and results are cached for a few minutes. The calculator and comparison are free; alerts, watchlist and a store data feed are a waitlist.

What it does not show:

- **It is not evidence that this ranks.** The sitemap lists 6 URLs, every one dated 2026-11-20, and the server's last-modified header is the same day, so the site was about one day old when read. Search results are drawn in the browser, so Google receives no per-sneaker page to rank. No traffic figure exists yet.
- **It is not evidence that naming stores is safe.** Mainstreet and Crepdog Crew both forbid automated access in their terms (section "Policy, law and store terms"). A one-day-old site has simply not been noticed yet.
- **Its position differs from the store's.** SoleSpread sells no shoes, so it is a neutral tool. The store is a competing seller, which is what turns a price row into comparative advertising.

What it confirms: matching by manufacturer style code and UK size is the workable method, which is the same prerequisite this report sets for phase 2, and a visible "fetched at" time with a short cache is how it handles stale prices. Its landed-cost calculator is also a page type the store could build with no scraping at all, from duty and tax rules plus its own prices, and the store's Search Console already shows the query shape ("onitsuka tiger price in japan vs india"). The duty rates SoleSpread quotes are for commercial imports and were not checked here.
