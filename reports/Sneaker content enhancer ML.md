# Build a Fact Engine, Not a Humanizer

SNKRS CART's enhancement stage should mostly verify facts and add India data, with prose polish a smaller part, because the evidence for 2025 and 2026 says Google rewards pages that carry something competitors cannot copy. It does not reward pages that merely read as human. The September 2025 Quality Rater Guidelines tell raters to give the Lowest rating to pages that are "all or almost all" AI-generated or paraphrased with "little to no added value", whatever tool made them, and to judge scaled abuse "after looking at several pages on the website" ([QRG PDF](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)). Meanwhile 92.9% of core-update winners in a 400-site study owned proprietary assets such as data, products or images, and pure news and affiliate publishers lost the most ([Practical Ecommerce](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/organic-search-winners-share-5-traits)). This matters for SNKRS CART because it already holds what Sneaker News, Hypebeast and Culture Circle lack: INR prices per size, seller offers, IST drop dates and stock state. No ranking competitor page measured on 2026-10-07 had an INR price table, a size table or a spec table for Indian buyers. The recommended design is a TypeScript stage that sits between the existing skills and `verifyBlogs.ts`. It scores the draft with deterministic checks, checks every date, price and style code against brand feeds and Indian retailer JSON, injects first-party data blocks, has Claude Code rewrite only the flagged sections, and sends the result to an independent judge from a different model family. Expected running cost is roughly $10 to $40 a month plus two $50 data prepays, against $299 a month for the cheapest Surfer plan with an API. Fine-tuning should wait until about 1,000 human-approved before-and-after pairs exist. The bigger near-term risk is not prose style but the unattended `auto-content.sh` pipeline: it produces thin drops (1 to 2 sentence descriptions) and short blogs (237 posts averaging 831 words, minimum 274), which is the sitewide pattern raters are told to treat as scaled content.

## Google now rewards effort, owned data and task completion over prose style

Google retired the helpful content system as a standalone classifier in March 2024 and folded it into core ranking, so every core update now re-scores helpfulness. There were core updates in March, June and December 2025 and in March and May 2026, plus five spam updates ([ranking systems guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide); [Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history)). The rater guidelines are explicitly neutral about AI: "the use of Generative AI tools alone does not determine the level of effort or Page Quality rating". Paraphrase without added value is a different matter. The QRG defines it as content that "exists on other pages, with different wording, organization or phrasing", which describes a release post that restates the same Nike press release as fifty other sites ([QRG PDF](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf)). Effort in rater terms explicitly includes "building systems that power a webpage", so a live drop calendar with countdowns and calendar export counts as effort in a way text alone cannot. Enforcement has also hardened. Scaled content abuse manual actions with full deindexing were first documented on 2-3 June 2025 against sites using generative AI "at mass scale" ([Gagan Ghotra](https://gaganghotra.com/blog/seo/scaled-content-abuse-manual-actions-google-issuing/)). Recovery is rare: only 22% of sites hit by the September 2023 helpful content update showed even a 20% lift ([The STACC](https://thestacc.com/blog/helpful-content-update-recovery-study/)). Avoiding a site-level demotion is much cheaper than recovering from one.

The studies on AI text and rank disagree, and the disagreement is informative. Ahrefs scored 600,000 top-20 pages and found a correlation of only 0.011 between AI share and position ([Ahrefs](https://www.ahrefs.com/blog/ai-generated-content-does-not-hurt-your-google-rankings)). Graphite found that AI pages made up 14% of results but only 7% of number-one results ([Graphite](https://graphite.io/five-percent/ai-content-in-search-and-llms)). Both can be true: AI-assisted pages are tolerated across the top 20, while the top slot goes to the page with new information. Google's information-gain patent (US11354342B2) scores a document by what it adds "beyond information contained in other documents that were already presented to the user" ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/information-gain/)). Raptive's analysis of the December 2025 update found that winners had under 7% of their pages at 500 words or fewer, against 32% or more for losers. Winners also had fresher content (393 days average age against 500) and higher branded search share ([Raptive](https://raptive.com/blog/what-googles-december-2025-core-update-tells-us-about-quality/)). By that standard, any SNKRS CART blog under 500 words is a sitewide liability, not just a weak page.

Two shifts in distribution favour an Indian store over US media. AI Overviews cut position-one CTR by 58% when present ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update)), and AI Mode sessions end without an external click 92 to 94% of the time ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-ai-mode-seo-impact/)). However, AI Overviews trigger on only about 3.2% of shopping queries and around 15% of news queries ([Serps.io](https://serps.io/blog/ai-overview-prevalence-by-industry)). Price, availability and release-date pages are the least exposed formats, and generic "what is the Air Jordan 1" explainers are the most exposed. HubSpot learned this when its blog lost 70 to 80% of traffic on generic informational content ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/hubspot-launches-aeo-tool-as-organic-traffic-drops-27-for-its-customers/)). The second shift is the February 2026 Discover core update, which favours "websites based in their country" and "in-depth, original, and timely content" from sites with topic expertise. It is rolling out to "all countries and languages" ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update)). Discover needs no application, only indexing, images at least 1,200 px wide with `max-image-preview:large`, and non-clickbait titles ([Discover docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)). Freshness counts only when it is real. Google says to update `dateModified` for substantive changes, and John Mueller says date changes without content changes do nothing ([publication dates](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/publication-dates); [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-john-mueller-updating-xml-sitemap-dates-doesnt-help-seo/545547/)). A drop moving from rumoured to confirmed to released to sold out is exactly that kind of substantive change.

One limit applies to all of this. Every CTR and core-update study cited here is US- or EU-centric, and no India-specific AI Overview trigger data was found. Treat the direction as reliable and the magnitudes as approximate for Indian searchers.

## Competitors leave India price, sizing and spec data uncontested

Global leaders run two templates. The first is a fast single-release post of 140 to 720 words with a spec block (colourway, style code, date, price, where to buy), 9 to 27 images and 27 to 45 internal links. The second is an evergreen calendar hub whose `dateModified` is updated daily, such as Sneaker Bar Detroit's 2,867-word page first published in 2014 ([Sneaker Bar Detroit](https://sneakerbardetroit.com/sneaker-release-dates/); [Nice Kicks](https://www.nicekicks.com/air-jordan-1-high-og-royal-iq5495-005/)). Their schema is inconsistent. Sneaker Freaker emits no JSON-LD at all ([Sneaker Freaker](https://www.sneakerfreaker.com/features/every-one-piece-sneaker-collaboration)), Nice Kicks uses ItemPage with no author, and one ranking Complex page has the `<title>` "Comments" ([Complex](https://www.complex.com/sneakers/a/victor-deng/air-jordan-4-flight-club-release-date-im4002-100)). Across the 17 competitor pages measured on 2026-10-07, none carried INR, a comparison or size table, or a visible FAQ.

India-modified queries are barely contested. For "Nike Dunk Low Panda price in India" the engine returned parasite pages on hijacked subdomains, StockX UK and one 2023 Mainstreet post of 372 words with zero INR figures ([Mainstreet](https://marketplace.mainstreet.co.in/blogs/news/what-makes-the-nike-dunk-low-panda-special)). "Adidas Samba vs Gazelle" surfaced a 2023 Laced post of 368 words with no H2s and no meta description ([Laced](https://blog.laced.com/adidas-samba-vs-adidas-gazelle-whats-the-difference/)). Google India autocomplete shows dense demand for exactly the store's models. Examples include "jordan 1 indian rupees today", "new balance 9060 india price in rupees", "crocs price in india under 2000", "is nike cheaper in dubai than india" and "import duty on shoes from usa to india" ([Google Suggest, gl=in](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&hl=en&gl=in&q=jordan%204%20price%20in%20india)). There is also a uniquely Indian angle that no global page covers. Since 22 September 2025, GST on footwear is 5% up to ₹2,500 a pair and 18% above it ([PIB GST reform](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/sep/doc202595629301.pdf)).

Culture Circle is the only Indian competitor running a real SEO programme. It publishes about 32 "{model} price India" posts with named authors, BlogPosting schema and metas such as "real prices from ₹9,899", at 620 to 1,130 words, with no tables, style codes, release dates or size charts ([Culture Circle](https://www.culture-circle.com/blogs/new-balance-9060-price-india-where-to-buy)). VegNonVeg puts its generic site title and meta on every blog post ([VegNonVeg](https://www.vegnonveg.com/blog/adidas-samba-from-football-roots-to-everyday-essential)). Superkicks and Crepdog Crew blogs are stale ([Superkicks sitemap](https://www.superkicks.in/sitemap_blogs_1.xml); [Crepdog sitemap](https://crepdogcrew.com/sitemap_blogs_1.xml)). SNKRS CART's own Last Dance post already beats them on schema and INR depth (NewsArticle plus Speakable, 11 INR mentions). It falls short on 3 images, 4 internal links, an organisation byline instead of a person, and no table ([SNKRS CART post](https://www.snkrscart.com/blogs/air-jordan-1-low-og-last-dance-at-the-garden-india-2026)). These gaps map directly onto enhancement rules. Every blog should get a spec and INR table, a named human byline, 15 or more internal links (product, drop, model hub, sibling posts) and more original images. Every sneaker profile should grow toward the 2,000 to 2,500 word model hub that ranks for "Air Jordan 1 guide" queries, adding the UK/US/EU size table and High/Mid/Low comparison that the Stadium Goods and Complex hubs both lack ([Stadium Goods](https://www.stadiumgoods.com/blogs/news/air-jordan-1-guide-history-different-heights-sizing); [Complex](https://www.complex.com/style/how-to-wear-air-jordan-1-guide)).

A caveat on these observations: the search engine used to observe SERPs was US-based, not google.co.in, so positions need re-checking with `gl=in` before content is prioritised.

## GSC plus a $100 data prepay covers India keyword research and measurement

India keyword data needs three layers: free first-party data, cheap resold Google data, and free demand probes. The first layer is the Search Console API. It is free and authoritative for queries the site already ranks for, with a `country equals ind` filter, 16 months of history, a quota of 1,200 queries per minute per site, and `type: discover` and `type: googleNews` switches. It returns only "top rows" and anonymises long-tail queries, so Hinglish queries will be under-counted ([searchanalytics.query](https://developers.google.com/webmaster-tools/v1/searchanalytics/query); [limits](https://developers.google.com/webmaster-tools/limits)). Discover data is page-level only, with no query or position dimensions ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2021/10/search-analytics-discover-gnews?hl=de)). The second layer covers keywords not yet ranked. DataForSEO is the budget answer: a $50 minimum prepay, Google Ads search volume at $0.075 per request of up to 1,000 keywords with India location 2356 and Hindi supported, autocomplete at $0.002 per call, and Labs keyword suggestions with search intent labels at about $0.01 ([DataForSEO pricing](https://dataforseo.com/pricing); [Ads volume docs](https://docs.dataforseo.com/v3/keywords_data/google_ads/search_volume/live/); [Labs docs](https://docs.dataforseo.com/v3/dataforseo_labs/google/keyword_suggestions/live/)). Serper adds India SERPs with People Also Ask and related searches at $50 for 50,000 queries valid for six months ([serper.dev](https://serper.dev/)). The direct Keyword Planner API is free but needs a Google Ads account, a developer token at Basic access and brand verification, and it returns bucketed 12-month averages that suit spiky drop demand poorly ([access levels](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/access-levels)). For rising queries around a drop, the official Trends API is still an allowlisted alpha without related queries, and pytrends was archived in April 2025. The DataForSEO or SerpApi Trends endpoints are the workable routes ([dev.to](https://dev.to/radevicb/google-trends-still-has-no-api-in-2026-heres-what-i-use-instead-1840); [DataForSEO Trends](https://docs.dataforseo.com/v3/keywords_data/google_trends/explore/live/)). Free probes include `suggestqueries.google.com` with `gl=in` (undocumented and blockable, with DataForSEO autocomplete as fallback) and Bing Webmaster's keyed `GetKeyword` API with country parameters ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/microsoft.bing.webmaster.api.interfaces.iwebmasterapi?view=bing-webmaster-dotnet)). Semrush ($549 a month for API) and Ahrefs ($129 and up with opaque units) are not justified until the pipeline proves out ([Ahrefs pricing](https://ahrefs.com/pricing)).

Keyword choice should be a scored, clustered brief, not a guess inside the skill. No opportunity formula has been validated against traffic, and vendors admit "there is no universal formula" ([Rankability](https://help.rankability.com/researcher/keyword-opportunities-and-scoring)). A transparent version is enough: log-scaled India volume, multiplied by an intent weight, catalogue relevance to the five store brands and (1 minus difficulty), with a boost for a drop date within 30 days and for a GSC striking-distance position of roughly 8 to 25. Clustering should use SERP overlap. Keywords sharing 7 or more of the top 10 URLs belong on one page, and those sharing 4 to 6 belong in the same cluster ([OpenSEO](https://instagit.com/every-app/open-seo/keyword-clustering-algorithm-similarity-metrics/)). Clustering 1,000 keywords costs about $1 of Serper calls. The same check stops cannibalisation: before a skill writes a new post, the brief should test whether an existing blog, drop or profile already owns that SERP, and recommend a refresh instead.

Measurement should compare cohorts at fixed checkpoints, because single pages are noisy and failures show up late. GSC fresh data lands about 12 hours after the day and is roughly 93% final at 36 hours ([Google, 2019](https://webmasters.googleblog.com/2019/09/search-performance-fresh-data.html)). Snapshots at day 7 (indexed, any impressions), day 28 (impressions, clicks, average position) and day 90 are reliable if taken two days after each window closes. Day 90 is essential. SE Ranking's unedited AI sites indexed fine and earned impressions in 36 days, then collapsed from 28% to 3% of pages in the top 100 by months 2 to 3. Its six edited, fact-checked and disclosed articles earned 555,000 impressions over 13 months, with 3 of 6 in the top 10 ([SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/ai-content-experiment/)). The Search Console Generative AI report added in June 2026 shows AI Overview and AI Mode impressions only, with no clicks, so treat it as a secondary signal ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-aio-track-visibility/560470/)). Keep pinging IndexNow through the existing `indexNow.ts`, but Google does not participate, so it only speeds up Bing and Yandex. Never send blogs through the Google Indexing API, which is limited to JobPosting and livestream pages and can be revoked for abuse ([Indexing API](https://developers.google.com/search/apis/indexing-api/v3/quickstart)).

## Humanness is measurable with a feature vector and a calibrated judge, but detectors make poor gates

AI detectors should not be the pass/fail gate. Vendor benchmarks claim sub-1% false-positive rates. In the field, commercial detectors flagged 9 to 15% of untouched 2023 to 2025 human abstracts, while Undetectable AI humanisation cut detection of AI rewrites to under 4% ([Karr et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11256)). Thirteen detectors run on professionally edited non-native manuscripts produced false-positive rates "from 0.0% to 100.0%" ([Park et al., EMNLP 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26710)). Commercial humanizers make the wrong trade. They beat perplexity-style detectors by scrambling vocabulary and syntax, which hurts meaning, and Pangram's model trained on 19 humanizer tools still catches them ([DAMAGE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03437v1); [BFI](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Artificial-Writing-and-Automated-Detection.pdf)). Expert readers also caught 100% of prompt-humanised articles ([Russell et al., ACL 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15654v1)). Google's own guidance never mentions detection. It asks publishers to "manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content" and to consider disclosing how content was made ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)).

Readers matter too, though not in the way often assumed. Blind, lay readers cannot reliably spot AI prose and often rate it equal or higher. Labelled or suspected AI triggers a penalty: 52% of consumers say they disengage when they suspect AI ([Bynder](https://www.bynder.com/en/press-media/ai-vs-human-made-content-study/); [Time on the Villanova study](https://time.com/article/2026/08/04/is-ai-telling-better-stories-than-us/)). Heavy LLM users detect AI at 92.7% with a 3.3% false-positive rate, so they are the real audience to satisfy. Sneaker-forum readers probably include many of them. What these readers notice is well documented. Vocabulary accounts for 53% of expert clues, sentence structure 36%, punctuation 25%, originality 24% and quotes 22% ([Russell et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15654v1)). The vocabulary excess is measured at corpus scale. "Delves" appeared at 28 times its expected frequency in 2024 PubMed abstracts ([Kobak et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016v5)), and GPT-4o uses "tapestry" 155 times and "camaraderie" 162 times more than humans. Instruction-tuned models also overuse participial clauses at 2 to 5 times the human rate and nominalisations at 1.5 to 2 times ([Reinhart et al., PNAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v2)). The word list drifts by model era: "delve" and "tapestry" faded after mid-2024, while "highlighting", "showcasing" and "emphasizing" took over ([Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). A static banned-phrase list like the one in `verifyBlogs.ts` therefore goes stale. Grammatical tells are more durable: participial tails, copula avoidance ("serves as" in place of "is"), negative parallelism ("not X, but Y") and summary-style closers.

The measurable version has three layers. Layer one is deterministic and costs nothing. For each article it computes sentence-length coefficient of variation, MTLD lexical diversity (not raw type-token ratio, which is length-sensitive), proper-noun and number density, mean sentence-to-sentence embedding similarity, participial-tail and nominalisation rates, the copula ratio, em dashes per 1,000 words, contraction and first-person counts, and tell hits per 1,000 words. Each value is normalised against a human baseline, and the tell list is regenerated every quarter with the open Kobak method ([berenslab/llm-excess-vocab](https://github.com/berenslab/llm-excess-vocab)). These are the features that separate human from LLM text at 87% accuracy in a 250-feature model and 93 to 98% with Biber features ([arXiv 2412.03025](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03025v1); [Reinhart](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v2)). The baseline must come from human-written sneaker and streetwear editorial, because feature distributions shift sharply by genre. No published Indian English reference values exist, so SNKRS CART has to build its own baseline from about 50 to 100 human-written Indian sneaker articles. Layer two is a pairwise LLM judge with a merged chain-of-thought and rubric prompt, run twice with positions swapped. The June 2026 judge audit found that style bias dominates: judges preferred markdown over plain prose 73 to 97% of the time, against 57% for humans. Markdown should therefore be stripped before judging. This setup reached about 71% human agreement (κ 0.55) at roughly $0.001 per evaluation on Gemini Flash. Claude Sonnet showed the least verbosity bias ([Soumik 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23178)). Because judges favour their own model family, a Claude-written article should be judged by Gemini, whose keys the project already holds. Layer three is calibration. Two humans label 100 to 400 article pairs, the judge and thresholds are tuned to that set, and the set is re-validated every quarter. Expect 70 to 80% agreement at best, which is also the human-to-human ceiling ([MT-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)). Disagreements go to a human reviewer.

The techniques that actually move a skilled reader are content changes, not obfuscation: real named retailers, INR figures, sizes and dates; real attributed quotes using "said"; an opinion; a closing paragraph that adds something instead of restating. Indian English spelling and local nouns also raise rare-word and proper-noun density, which moves every metric toward the human side ([Liang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819)). Critique-and-revise loops improve human preference by about 20 points with no training ([Self-Refine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)). No study has measured them against detectors, which is fine for a pipeline that does not treat detectors as the target.

## Facts resolve against style codes, brand feeds and Indian retailer JSON

No single India-aware sneaker fact API exists, but a tiered source stack does. The strongest is Nike's undocumented, unauthenticated `api.nike.com/product_feed/threads/v2`. Its India web channel returned 15,299 threads with INR full and current price, style code, colour description and `commerceStartDate`, for example "Air Jordan 1 High OG 'Khaki' | FD2596-201 | 16995 INR" (own fetch, 2026-10-07, documented in the research notes). Being unauthenticated does not make it licensed, so use it to verify facts, not to republish content. Four Indian Shopify stores expose `/products.json` with INR prices and availability. Superkicks and Limited Edt SKUs follow a `STYLECODE-SIZE` pattern that the existing scraper already ingests, Crepdog Crew carries the code in `body_html`, Myntra and Flipkart are server-rendered HTML, and adidas India and Ajio sit behind Akamai. Shopify's `updated_at` changes daily on every product, so price changes must be detected by diffing snapshots. Sole Retriever blocks HTML fetches, but its open `sitemap-products.xml` lists about 10,000 release slugs with embedded style codes and same-day `lastmod` ([Sole Retriever sitemap](https://www.soleretriever.com/sitemap-products.xml)). That is enough to confirm that a style code exists and when its page last changed. StockX's official API is gated to approved sellers, KicksDB starts at €29 a month for resale prices in USD and EUR ([kicks.dev](https://kicks.dev/)), and Sneaks-API has been dead since February 2023 ([GitHub](https://github.com/druv5319/Sneaks-API)). Wikidata is unsafe for anything beyond brand inception years. Both the Air Jordan and Dunk "designer Peter Moore" claims link to the wrong people ([Wikidata Q420953](https://www.wikidata.org/w/api.php?action=wbgetentities&ids=Q420953&props=claims|labels|sitelinks&languages=en&format=json)).

The verifier should work like FActScore and SAFE. It splits the draft into atomic claims, such as a date, a price, a style code, a colourway name, a retailer or a historical year, then labels each one supported, unsupported or unverifiable. SAFE agreed with human annotators 72% of the time and won 76% of the cases where they disagreed, at more than 20 times lower cost ([SAFE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802)). Claims should be joined on style code, never on name, since Nike and retailers name the same shoe differently. The source order runs from the Nike India feed, to the Nike US feed, to Indian retailer JSON, to the Sole Retriever sitemap, to RSS from Sneaker Bar Detroit, Sneaker News, Hypebeast and Nice Kicks ([SBD feed](https://sneakerbardetroit.com/feed/)), and finally to other blogs. Within a tier, the newest statement wins. A lower tier overrides a higher tier only if it is materially newer and names a delay explicitly. Dates that differ across tiers by more than a few days become flagged conflicts, not silent fixes. USD retail and INR MRP are separate market-scoped facts, not a conflict. The cost of skipping this step is documented: CNET found errors in 41 of 77 AI articles, including $10,300 of interest where $300 was correct ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/cnet-reviewing-ai-written-articles-serious-errors-113041405.html)). The project's own collections should be checked first, because `Product`, `Drop` and `SneakerProfile` already hold most claims. This requires one schema change: `Drop` needs a `styleCode` field, without which no drop claim can be joined to any external source. Images need a matching rule. Nike's terms license its content "for personal, non-commercial use" only, and Indian fair dealing is narrower than US fair use ([Nike ToS archive](https://www.toolongdidntread.it.com/companies/nike/terms/v/20240705_rev01); [BananaIP](https://bananaip.com/ip-news-center/copyrights-fair-dealing-fair-use-india)). The enhancer should therefore prefer photos of stock the store actually holds and seller verification photos, which are also the information-gain images competitors cannot copy.

Structured data has narrowed, and the enhancer should emit only what still pays. FAQ rich results ended for every site on 7 May 2026. HowTo has left the gallery. Event markup is explicitly disallowed for product sales and drops, and ItemList carousels render only for courses, movies, recipes and restaurants ([SEJ on FAQ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-drops-faq-rich-results-from-search/574429/); [Event](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/event); [Carousel](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/carousel)). The existing FAQPage JSON-LD on `/drops` is harmless, but it no longer earns a rich result. The visible FAQ copy remains useful for AI Mode's query fan-out. What remains worth emitting is listed below.

| Page | Emit | Key rules |
|---|---|---|
| Blog | NewsArticle or BlogPosting, BreadcrumbList | `author` as a Person with `url` to an author page; `datePublished`/`dateModified` in ISO 8601 with +05:30; images in 16x9, 4x3 and 1x1 at 1,200 px or more ([Article docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article)) |
| Drop detail | Product (merchant listing only if buyable on site), BreadcrumbList | Style code in `mpn`, `offers.priceCurrency` INR, `availability` PreOrder only if real; never Event ([merchant listing](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing)) |
| Calendar hub | CollectionPage, BreadcrumbList, ItemList for crawl only | `dateModified` computed from the newest drop `updatedAt`, not render time |
| Sneaker profile | Article plus BreadcrumbList | Links down to every colourway product and drop, and across to sibling models |
| Site | Organization with `sameAs` and return policy, WebSite SearchAction | Return policy nested under Organization ([Product intro](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product)) |

## Build the stage, buy the data, defer fine-tuning

Commercial optimisers are not worth their price here. Surfer, Clearscope, Frase and NeuronWriter all score term coverage against the top 10 to 20 pages. Ahrefs found "weak correlations everywhere" between those scores and rankings, with Surfer, Frase and Clearscope "very weak" ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-content-score-study/)). The cheapest Surfer plan with an API costs $299 a month ([Surfer pricing](https://surferseo.com/pricing/)). The same coverage checklist costs under one cent per article to build in-house: one Serper query, fetching 10 pages through the existing `stealthGet`, BM25 term gaps with `fast-bm25`, entity gaps through Dandelion's 1,000 free daily units, and embeddings at $0.02 per million tokens ([Dandelion](https://dandelion.eu/profile/plans-and-pricing/); [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)). None of the open-source research pipelines (gpt-researcher, STORM) scores against the live SERP or gates on verified claims, and STORM's authors say its output "cannot produce publication-ready articles" ([STORM](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm)). Building is both cheaper and a better fit.

Fine-tuning should wait. Training is cheap: Fireworks LoRA costs $0.50 per million training tokens, so the whole roughly 450,000-token corpus over 3 epochs costs under $1 ([Fireworks](https://fireworks.ai/pricing)). Every other factor argues against it. Gemini API tuning was removed in May 2025, and OpenAI is winding down its fine-tuning platform ([Google](https://ai.google.dev/docs/model_tuning_guidance); [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)). A 2026 single-author LoRA study on 487 paragraphs removed 76 to 91% of AI tells but never recovered personal voice, with authorship probability stuck at 0.51 to 0.53 ([InMyStyle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29238)). Fine-tuning on facts the base model lacks "linearly increase[s] the model's tendency to hallucinate" ([Gekhman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05904)), which is dangerous for a fast-moving release catalogue. By contrast, a cached 30,000-token style guide with exemplars costs about $0.006 per call on Claude Sonnet 5.5 or Opus 5.5 ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Revisit fine-tuning when one of three triggers is met: about 1,000 human-approved before-and-after pairs exist, monthly volume passes about 1,000 enhancement passes, or offline drafting on an M-series Mac becomes the goal. If it does happen, use a LoRA on a 3B to 8B open model with named entities and numbers masked from the loss, as StyleTunedLM did ([arXiv 2409.04574](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04574v1)).

The architecture below fits the current pipeline. The skills already run inside Claude Code, so the expensive rewrite step can stay in the skill session at no extra API cost. A new backend script produces a structured enhancement report the skill must act on, and only the independent judge and data calls go to paid APIs. `verifyBlogs.ts` stays as the final mechanical gate and gets sibling checks for drops and profiles.

```

   |
 [0] Brief (before writing) ... GSC striking distance + DataForSEO volume/intent + Serper gl=in SERP/PAA;

 [1] Draft (skill writes HTML / Drop / SneakerProfile, published:false)
   |

   |   b. first-party injection list: INR offers table, IST drop time, seller count, size run
   |   c. SERP gap: BM25 terms + entities vs top 10 India pages; heading/length targets
   |   d. style vector + tell lint vs human sneaker baseline; duplicate cosine vs own corpus

 [3] Revise (same Claude Code session): rewrite flagged sections only, insert data blocks
 [4] Judge (Gemini, different family): pairwise old vs new, swapped order, markdown stripped
 [5] Gate: verifyBlogs.ts / verifyDrops.ts / verifySneakers.ts + 0 unsupported claims
   |       + judge win + dup cosine < 0.85 -> draft branch; else status "needs_review"
 [6] Store ContentEnhancement doc (draft, final, metrics, claims, verdicts) -> eval + future tuning

```

## Conclusion

The research changes the problem being solved. "Make AI content sound human" turns out to be the least valuable and least measurable goal, because blind readers cannot tell the difference, detectors are noisy in both directions, and Google says it does not judge production method. The thing Google demonstrably rewards is the thing SNKRS CART has and its competitors lack: verified, dated, India-specific commerce data attached to a store that lets people act on it. The enhancement stage should therefore be measured first by how many unsupported claims it blocks and how much first-party data it adds per page, and only second by its style scores.

The most consequential action costs nothing. The unattended generator already produces drops with 1 to 2 sentence descriptions and blogs as short as 274 words, which is the sitewide pattern raters are told to treat as scaled content and which almost never recovers once demoted. Capping auto-publication, refreshing or merging the thin back catalogue, and requiring every new drop to carry a style code, INR price and Indian retailer before it is indexed will protect rankings more than any rewrite model. Every approved enhancement also becomes a labelled training pair, so the decision on fine-tuning can be made later on the site's own evidence.
